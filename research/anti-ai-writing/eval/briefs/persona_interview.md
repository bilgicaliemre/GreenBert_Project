# FICTIONAL student interview notes (test material only; this person does not exist)

Student: Inês Carvalho, 17, senior, Fall River, Massachusetts. Raw answers from a 40-minute brainstorm interview,
lightly cleaned. She talks fast and corrects herself. Use ONLY these facts; anything not here is unknown.

Q: What do you do on a normal Saturday?
A: I work at my dad's shoe repair shop, Carvalho Shoe Service, on Pleasant Street. It's been there since 1994. I do
the counter mostly, tickets, and since last summer I'm allowed to do heels and half-soles on the finishing machine.
The shop smells like Barge cement and leather dust, my hoodie smells like it too, my friends say I smell like a
"cowboy's basement". I hate the phone. I have a stutter that gets worse on the phone, especially on hard sounds
like "C", which is a problem because the shop is called Carvalho. So I used to say "Hi, shoe repair" instead of the
name. My dad noticed after like a year and never said anything about it.

Q: What's something you're weirdly good at?
A: I can tell how someone walks from the wear on their heel. Outer edge worn = they supinate. One lady's right heel
was ground down way more than the left and it turned out she drives for work, the right heel rests on the floor by
the pedal. I said it out loud and she looked at me like I was a psychic. I keep a notebook of heels. It has 61
entries. Entry 1 is my dad's work boots.

Q: Tell me about a time you were wrong.
A: Sophomore year I got a 54 on my first AP Chem test and I told my mom it was an 85. I kept it up for three weeks,
I even forged the parent signature on the progress report, it was really bad. My teacher Mr. Oduya emailed home.
My mom didn't yell, she just asked me to explain the test to her, question by question, at the kitchen table, in
Portuguese, which I'm not good at for chemistry words. It took two hours. I didn't know the word for "mole" so I
said "the unit thing, like a dozen". She laughed at that part. I ended with a B+ in the class. After that I made a
rule: I keep a list on my phone of every lie I tell, even small ones like "I'm almost there". The list has 212
items since October of sophomore year. Most of them are about being on time. I'm still not sure if the list makes
me more honest or just better at noticing.

Q: Who do you spend time with that people wouldn't expect?
A: My avó, my grandmother, Rosa. She had a stroke two years ago and has aphasia. She understands more than she can
say. She calls me Lurdes sometimes, which was my aunt's name, who died before I was born. At first I corrected her,
now I don't. I made flashcards for her with pictures, in Portuguese, and we do them Tuesday and Thursday after
school. It's funny because she's relearning words and I'm learning them for the first time; she's better than me
at "agulha" (needle), I'm better at "telemóvel" (cell phone). She can't say my name yet. She can say "sapato".

Q: What do you want to study?
A: Speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics. Or something with how the brain gets words back. I read a lot
about aphasia now. I found out stuttering and aphasia are totally different things, which I kind of wanted to be
connected and they're not. I asked my speech therapist from middle school, Ms. Halvorsen, if I'd be "allowed" to be
a speech therapist with a stutter and she said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.

Q: Anything else? Small things.
A: I can resole a sneaker in 25 minutes. I have never once answered the shop phone with the name. Last month I did,
actually, I said "Carvalho Shoe Service" on the third try, the guy waited. It was a guy asking if we fix purses. We
don't. I also do Science Olympiad (forensics event), I'm treasurer of Portuguese Club, I have a 3.9 GPA, I run
cross-country badly (my best 5K is 24:40). My dad's hands are stained black on the right thumb from the dye. I
asked him once if he wanted me to take over the shop and he said "the shop isn't a person, it doesn't need anyone."
I don't know if that was a yes or a no.
