# GreenBERT facts (the ONLY facts a writer may use for GreenBERT tasks)
- GreenBERT is a high-school research project (first author a high-school student, with a university co-author) that detects possible greenwashing in corporate ESG (environmental, social, governance) reports.
- Stage built so far: rule-based claim extraction. A PDF report is converted to text with page numbers (PyMuPDF), split into sentences (NLTK).
- A sentence passes the "gate" only if it contains an ESG topic word AND either an action verb (including first-person and past forms such as "we reduced") or a hard quantity (numbers with units, counts). A topic word alone is treated as description and dropped.
- Then non-claim filters remove: boilerplate, reference-dense text, navigation and headers, broken fragments (checked with a part-of-speech tagger for a verb), policy content, governance role descriptions, activity reports, methodology text, general statements and risk descriptions. Every rejected sentence is saved with its drop reason so recall can be audited.
- Each claim is labelled E, S or G by keyword dominance; Claim_Type is Future Promise, Strong (hard quantity) or Vague; Evidence is Yes / Partial / No; plus a Risk_Signal.
- Output for one Unilever report: 583 claims; Dr Pepper report: 218 claims. Six reports are loaded in total.
- Evaluation (all in-sample, so optimistic): a 26-sentence development set reached 92% precision (filters were tuned on it, so it is not a headline). An independent 100-claim manual review of an earlier version (v3) found 67% precision. Automated full-corpus audits by AI agents (stricter than humans) gave precision 33% (v3), 39% (v4.0), 44% (v4 final). Recall, estimated by auditing 1,767 rejected sentences, rose from 48% (v4.0) to 73% (v4 final).
- Key insight: keyword rules hit a semantic ceiling; residual errors are about meaning, which motivates the next stage, a BERT classifier (not built yet). The end goal is a public website where someone uploads an ESG report PDF and gets a greenwashing assessment with an explanation.
- Example of a vague claim the system flags: "We are committed to a sustainable future." Example of a strong claim: "We reduced Scope 1 and 2 emissions by 4% against our 2019 baseline."
