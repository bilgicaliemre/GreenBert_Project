# Question: Does Romeo and Juliet blame fate or the characters' own haste for the lovers' deaths?
# Quotes from Romeo and Juliet by William Shakespeare (c. 1595, public domain; Project Gutenberg eBook #1513) that a writer MAY quote. Do not quote anything else. A " / " marks a verse line break; quote it as " / " or set the lines as block verse.
Q1 (Prologue, the Chorus): "From forth the fatal loins of these two foes / A pair of star-cross’d lovers take their life; / Whose misadventur’d piteous overthrows / Doth with their death bury their parents’ strife."
Q2 (Act II, Scene 3, Romeo asks Friar Lawrence to marry him to Juliet the same day; the Friar answers): "O let us hence; I stand on sudden haste." / "Wisely and slow; they stumble that run fast."
Q3 (Act III, Scene 1, Romeo, just after killing Tybalt): "O, I am fortune’s fool!"
Q4 (Act V, Scene 1, Romeo, on hearing from Balthasar that Juliet is dead): "Is it even so? Then I defy you, stars! / Thou know’st my lodging. Get me ink and paper, / And hire post-horses. I will hence tonight."
