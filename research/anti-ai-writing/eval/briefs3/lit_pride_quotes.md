# Question: How does Elizabeth Bennet's first impression of Mr. Darcy mislead her, and what does the novel suggest about judging people quickly?
# Quotes from Pride and Prejudice by Jane Austen (1813, public domain; Project Gutenberg eBook #1342) that a writer MAY quote. Do not quote anything else.
Q1 (Chapter 3, Darcy at the Meryton ball, overheard by Elizabeth, about her): "She is tolerable: but not handsome enough to tempt me; and I am in no humour at present to give consequence to young ladies who are slighted by other men."
Q2 (Chapter 34, Elizabeth refusing Darcy's first proposal): "From the very beginning, from the first moment, I may almost say, of my acquaintance with you, your manners impressing me with the fullest belief of your arrogance, your conceit, and your selfish disdain of the feelings of others, were such as to form that groundwork of disapprobation, on which succeeding events have built so immovable a dislike"
Q3 (Chapter 36, Elizabeth alone, after reading Darcy's letter): "How despicably have I acted! [...] I, who have prided myself on my discernment! [...] Pleased with the preference of one, and offended by the neglect of the other, on the very beginning of our acquaintance, I have courted prepossession and ignorance, and driven reason away where either were concerned. Till this moment, I never knew myself."
Q4 (Chapter 59, Elizabeth, half joking, when Jane asks how long she has loved Darcy): "It has been coming on so gradually, that I hardly know when it began; but I believe I must date it from my first seeing his beautiful grounds at Pemberley."
