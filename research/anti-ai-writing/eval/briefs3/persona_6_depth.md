## Part 1: brainstorm

Student: Marisol Ybarra, 17, senior at Tucson High, Tucson, Arizona.

Q: What do you do on a normal Saturday?
A: In monsoon season? Okay, so monsoon is officially June 15 to September 30, the weather service just picked dates
in 2008 because people kept fighting about dew points. (laughs) Sorry. So 6 a.m. I go up on the roof before it's 100
degrees, check my station, wipe pigeon stuff off the rain gauge. Noon to like 4 it's tamalada in our kitchen. My
mom, my Tía Bea, me, and my nana Chayo in a chair telling everybody the masa is too thick. Mine get redone. Tía Bea
just takes them off my pile without saying anything, which is worse. Summer we do the Saturday night market behind
the Mercado, 6 to 9, and Sundays it's Rillito, all year. I run the Square reader. Four dollars each, $42 a dozen,
green corn is $45 because the corn is a nightmare. Gabe, my brother, says $42 is "emotional pricing."

Q: What's something you're weirdly good at?
A: Guessing what people order from their shoes. Chacos, green corn. Cowboy boots, red chile pork. Crocs, cheese and
jalapeño, I can't explain it. I started bagging before people finished talking and my mom made me stop, because a
lady in Chacos wanted pork and said "you don't know me." (laughs) She was right, I didn't. Gabe says it's
confirmation bias, he took one psych class at Pima and now everything is confirmation bias. But like, I never
actually counted? Rain I write down to the hundredth of an inch, every time. The shoes I never wrote down once, I
just kind of believe it.

Q: Tell me about something you made.
A: The station. It's on our flat roof next to the swamp cooler. A Raspberry Pi my uncle Memo gave me, a BME280
sensor for temperature, humidity and pressure, that was $9, a tipping-bucket rain gauge for $31, and a lightning
sensor, $24, it beeps when a strike is within ten kilometers. The anemometer I made from ping-pong balls cut in half
and a bike speedometer magnet. It logs every minute to a Google Sheet. First month it said it rained 19 days in
June. It didn't rain once. It was the swamp cooler dripping in. I emailed the weather service office about my
"unusual totals" and a man named Glen wrote back one line: "Do you have an evaporative cooler?" (laughs) Also my
nana thinks the lightning sensor attracts lightning, so when it beeps she unplugs everything. My best storm this
year, August 9th, 1.62 inches in 38 minutes, has a 23-minute hole in the middle, that's her unplugging it.

Q: Tell me about a time you were wrong.
A: July 18th, night market. There was a cell over the Rincons and Gabe was working with us, which he never does.
Around 6:40 you could see dust coming from the east, brown, and he said let's pack up. I had radar open, I said it's
splitting, it's going north, trust me. I actually said "trust me." My mom laughed and said "la científica says we
stay." The canopy weights were my job, four sandbags, 25 pounds each, and they were still in the truck, because
since setup I'd been on my phone looking at radar. 6:52 the gust front hit. Our canopy went up like a kite and
cartwheeled two booths over into Mr. Duarte's honey table. Fourteen jars, $168. The steamer went over, six dozen in
the gravel. Nobody got hurt. A little girl with a mango paleta was right there and her dad yanked her by the arm.
Gabe didn't say anything, he just started picking up the glass. Honestly him yelling would've been easier? I don't
know.

Q: Who do you argue with?
A: Gabe. He's 24, he's an HVAC tech. He did the stand's numbers on our fridge whiteboard. Booth fee $45 a market,
masa, pork, husks, gas, and all our hours, and he says on a normal Sunday my mom makes $4.10 an hour. She has a disc
thing in her lower back. He wants her to stop the weekly markets and just do December orders, last year was 380
dozen, plus Instagram pre-orders. I keep saying people come for her. Mr. Hollis buys exactly seven every Sunday,
five years, won't tell us why. Gabe says "Mr. Hollis is not a business plan." (laughs) Which, okay. We had a real
fight in the truck at 6 a.m. on the way to Rillito and my mom turned Los Bukis up so loud we had to stop. And he's
right, about the money part. I still argue with him though.

Q: What do you want people to stop assuming about you?
A: That I want to be on TV. Every adult hears atmospheric science and goes "oh, like Channel 4!" and does the arm
thing at a green screen. No. And that the tamales are some ancient family recipe. The red chile is my nana's, yes.
The green corn my mom learned from Doña Ofelia at church, she's from Hermosillo and my mom is sure she leaves out
one step on purpose. The vegan ones are from a YouTube guy in LA. Also I don't love tamales. I eat a Hot Pocket in
the truck. Please don't put that anywhere.

Q: What do you want to study?
A: Atmospheric science. Or undecided, honestly, because I got a 3 on AP Calc AB and meteorology is calc and physics
all the way down. What I actually want to know is where monsoon storms start on a given afternoon. The models are
bad at it. Like around 3 o'clock everybody in Tucson is just staring at the mountains, it's kind of like a show. U of A has the program and I
could live at home, and Gabe says then I'll be at Rillito every Sunday till I'm 40. Oklahoma has a huge meteorology
school in Norman. My mom thinks Tornado Alley is a way of choosing to die.

Q: Anything else? Small things.
A: GPA 3.87 unweighted. SAT 1360, I keep saying I'll retake, I won't. My dad's a mail carrier, route in Midvale
Park, he's the only one who uses the station, he checks the heat index before work and calls it "Marisol's channel."
I post my data on Weather Underground. I have one follower who isn't family, AZdesertrat, he comments "nice" on
every storm. No idea who he is.

## Part 2: depth questions (second session)

Q: Walk me through July 18th again. What exactly happened, in order?
A: We set up at 5:30, it was 104. Gabe and I did the canopy legs, then I sat on the cooler with my phone because I'd
seen the cell over the Rincons on the drive. My mom said get the bags, I said "in a sec." Twice, I think. Then the
dust, then Gabe, then me with "trust me." And the thing is, the rain did go north. We got like four drops. My
forecast was kind of right? It was the outflow, the wind that runs out ahead of a storm, and that's literally what
I read about all summer. I was so locked on where the rain was going, the wind just wasn't in my head, at all.

Q: What were you thinking right when Gabe said pack up?
A: (pause) That if we left and it missed, that's one more point for his whiteboard. Like, see, the stand even loses
nights. And if we stayed and it missed, I'd be right in front of him. It wasn't really about customers or tamales or
whatever. I just wanted to win, basically. Ugh. Saying it out loud is so bad.

Q: What did you do right after?
A: Drove home with Gabe, nobody talking, and I went straight up on the roof to see what my station got. 47 miles an
hour. Which means nothing, our house is six miles from the Mercado. My mom was at the kitchen table going through
the husks, seeing if any were still okay. I don't think any were. And I was up on the roof with my graph, so. Yeah.

Q: What do you do differently now?
A: Sandbags first, before I even look at the sky. And I don't announce forecasts at the stand anymore. I still check
radar like every five minutes. I just don't say anything. So, I don't know. Maybe I changed, or maybe I just stopped saying it out loud. Probably
that one.

Q: What did you tell nobody?
A: That I'm at $60. My mom paid Mr. Duarte the $168 that night, cash, out of the apron, and I said I'd pay her
back. Gabe thinks I already did. She hasn't asked, not one time. Part of me wants her to ask, which is weird.

Q: Is Gabe right?
A: About the money, yeah. In the truck, the Los Bukis fight, he said I want the stand to stay exactly the same so I
can go away and it's still there when I come back, like my nana's chair. I said that was stupid.
(long pause) Like, I'm the one who's leaving, probably. And I'm also the one saying keep doing it, standing on the
gravel every Sunday, with her back. That doesn't, like. Yeah. I don't know. I don't know where I was going with that.

Q: What's still unresolved?
A: In September my mom asked me, just me, not Gabe, if she should drop the Saturday night market next summer. I said
I'd look at the numbers. And I did them, they're done, they're in my Google Sheet in a tab next to the rain stuff.
I just haven't said anything back to her yet. Also U of A or Norman, which she says she has no opinion on, so she does. And the $108.
