I used to answer the phone at my dad's shoe repair shop with "Hi, shoe repair." The shop has been on Pleasant Street since 1994. It smells like Barge cement and leather dust, and so does my hoodie (my friends say I smell like a "cowboy's basement"). On Saturdays I mostly do the counter and the tickets. Since last summer I've been allowed to do heels and half-soles on the finishing machine, and I can resole a sneaker in 25 minutes. From the wear on someone's heels I can tell how they walk, and I keep a notebook of heels; entry 1 is my dad's work boots. But I hate the phone. I have a stutter that gets worse on it, especially on hard sounds like "C," which is a problem, because the shop is called Carvalho Shoe Service. My dad noticed what I was doing after about a year and never said anything about it.

My avó, Rosa, had a stroke two years ago and has aphasia. She understands more than she can say. She can say sapato (shoe), but she can't say my name yet, and sometimes she calls me Lurdes, which was the name of my aunt, who died before I was born. At first I corrected her; now I don't. On Tuesdays and Thursdays after school we go through flashcards I made for her, with pictures, in Portuguese. It's funny, because she's relearning the words and I'm learning them for the first time. She's better than me at agulha (needle), and I'm better at telemóvel (cell phone).

Sophomore year I got a 54 on my first AP Chem test and told my mom it was an 85. I kept that up for three weeks and even forged the parent signature on the progress report, which was really bad. Then my teacher, Mr. Oduya, emailed home. My mom didn't yell. She asked me to explain the test to her, question by question, at the kitchen table, in Portuguese, which I'm not good at for chemistry words. It took two hours. I didn't know the word for "mole," so I said "the unit thing, like a dozen," and she laughed at that part. I finished the class with a B+. After that I made a rule: I keep a list on my phone of every lie I tell, even small ones like "I'm almost there." It has 212 items going back to October of sophomore year, and most of them are about being on time. I'm still not sure if the list makes me more honest or just better at noticing.

I read a lot about aphasia now. I found out that stuttering and aphasia are totally different things; I kind of wanted them to be connected, and they're not. I want to study speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics, or something with how the brain gets words back. When I asked Ms. Halvorsen, my speech therapist from middle school, if I'd be "allowed" to be a speech therapist with a stutter, she said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.

Last month, for the first time, I answered the shop phone with the name. "Carvalho Shoe Service" came out on the third try, and the man on the other end waited. He was calling to ask if we fix purses, which we don't.
