My grandmother Rosa can say "sapato" (shoe). She can't say my name yet. She had a stroke two years ago and has aphasia: she understands more than she can say. Sometimes she calls me Lurdes, which was the name of my aunt who died before I was born. At first I corrected her; now I don't. I made her flashcards with pictures, in Portuguese, and we do them Tuesday and Thursday after school. It's funny, because she's relearning the words and I'm learning them for the first time. She's better than me at "agulha" (needle), and I'm better at "telemóvel" (cell phone).

I have a stutter that gets worse on the phone, especially on hard sounds like "C," which is a problem because my dad's shoe repair shop is called Carvalho Shoe Service. I used to answer "Hi, shoe repair" instead of the name. Last month I said "Carvalho Shoe Service" on the third try, and the guy on the line waited. I read a lot about aphasia now. I kind of wanted it to be connected to my stutter, but they're totally different things.

I want to study speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics, or something with how the brain gets words back. I asked Ms. Halvorsen, my speech therapist from middle school, if I'd be "allowed" to be a speech therapist with a stutter. She said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.
