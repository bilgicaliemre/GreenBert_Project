I hate the phone. I have a stutter, and it gets worse on the phone, especially on hard sounds like C. That's a problem at my dad's shop, where I work on Saturdays, because the shop is called Carvalho Shoe Service and Carvalho is also my last name. So when the shop phone rang, I used to pick up and say, "Hi, shoe repair," which has no C in it. My dad, whose right thumb is stained black from the dye, noticed what I was doing after about a year and never said anything about it.

The shop has been on Pleasant Street since 1994. It smells like Barge cement and leather dust, and so does my hoodie; my friends say I smell like a cowboy's basement. I mostly do the counter and the tickets. Since last summer I've been allowed to do heels and half-soles on the finishing machine, and I can resole a sneaker in 25 minutes. I can also tell how someone walks from the wear on their heels. If the outer edge is worn down, they supinate (when they walk, their weight rolls onto the outside of the foot). One woman's right heel was ground down way more than her left, and it turned out she drives for work, so that heel rests on the floor by the pedal. I said it out loud, and she looked at me like I was a psychic. I keep a notebook of heels. It has 61 entries, and entry 1 is my dad's work boots.

My avó, my grandmother Rosa, calls me Lurdes sometimes. Lurdes was my aunt, who died before I was born. Avó had a stroke two years ago and has aphasia; she understands more than she can say. At first I corrected her, and now I don't. She can't say my name yet, but she can say sapato, the Portuguese word for shoe. On Tuesdays and Thursdays after school we go through the flashcards I made for her, with pictures, in Portuguese. It's funny, because she's relearning the words and I'm learning them for the first time. She's better than me at agulha (needle), and I'm better at telemóvel (cell phone).

I read a lot about aphasia now. One thing I found out is that stuttering and aphasia are totally different things. I kind of wanted them to be connected, and they're not. I want to study speech-language pathology, or maybe linguistics, or something with how the brain gets words back. When I asked Ms. Halvorsen, who was my speech therapist back in middle school, whether I'd be "allowed" to be a speech therapist with a stutter, she said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.

I asked my dad once if he wanted me to take over the shop someday. He said, "The shop isn't a person, it doesn't need anyone," and I haven't figured out whether that was a yes or a no. I had never once answered the shop phone with the name until last month, when I said "Carvalho Shoe Service" instead of "Hi, shoe repair." It took me three tries, and the man on the other end of the line waited through the first two. He was calling to ask if we fix purses, which we don't.
