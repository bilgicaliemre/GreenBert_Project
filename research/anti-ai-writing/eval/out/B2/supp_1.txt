On Tuesdays and Thursdays after school, my grandmother, Rosa, and I go through the picture flashcards I made for her in Portuguese. She had a stroke two years ago and has aphasia: she understands more than she can say. It's funny, because she's relearning the words and I'm learning them for the first time. She's better than me at "agulha" (needle); I'm better at "telemóvel" (cell phone). She can say "sapato" (shoe) but can't say my name yet.

I read a lot about aphasia now. I want to know how the brain gets words back. I have a stutter, and I kind of wanted stuttering and aphasia to be connected, but I found out they're totally different things.

My stutter gets worse on the phone, especially on hard sounds like C. That's a problem on Saturdays, when I work the counter at my dad's shoe repair shop, Carvalho Shoe Service. I used to answer with "Hi, shoe repair." My dad noticed after about a year and never said anything about it. Last month, a guy who wanted to know if we fix purses (we don't) waited while I said "Carvalho Shoe Service" on the third try.

In college I'd like to study speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics. I asked Ms. Halvorsen, my speech therapist from middle school, if I'd be "allowed" to be a speech therapist with a stutter, and she said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.
