I hate the phone. When it rang at my dad's shop, I used to pick it up and say, "Hi, shoe repair." My dad noticed after about a year and never said anything about it. The shop is Carvalho Shoe Service (Carvalho is my last name too), and it has been on Pleasant Street in Fall River since 1994. I have a stutter that gets worse on the phone, especially on hard sounds like the C in Carvalho.

The shop smells like Barge cement and leather dust, and so does my hoodie; my friends say I smell like a cowboy's basement. I'm there on Saturdays, mostly at the counter writing tickets, and since last summer I'm allowed to do heels and half-soles on the finishing machine. I can tell how someone walks from the wear on their heels: if the outer edge is worn down, they supinate. I keep a notebook of heels with 61 entries, and entry 1 is my dad's work boots.

Sophomore year I got a 54 on my first AP Chem test and told my mom it was an 85. I kept that up for three weeks. I even forged the parent signature on the progress report, which was really bad, and then my teacher, Mr. Oduya, emailed home. My mom didn't yell. She asked me to explain the test to her, question by question, at the kitchen table, in Portuguese, which I'm not good at for chemistry words. It took two hours. I didn't know the word for "mole," so I said "the unit thing, like a dozen," and she laughed at that part. After that I made a rule: I keep a list on my phone of every lie I tell, even small ones like "I'm almost there." I'm still not sure if the list makes me more honest or just better at noticing. It has 212 items going back to October of sophomore year, and most of them are about being on time.

On Tuesdays and Thursdays after school I do flashcards with my avó, my grandmother Rosa. She had a stroke two years ago and has aphasia, and she understands more than she can say. I made the cards for her, in Portuguese, with pictures. It's funny, because she's relearning the words and I'm learning them for the first time: she's better than me at "agulha" (needle), and I'm better at "telemóvel" (cell phone). Sometimes she calls me Lurdes, which was the name of my aunt who died before I was born. At first I corrected her, and now I don't. She can't say my name yet. She can say "sapato" (shoe).

I read a lot about aphasia now, and I found out that stuttering and aphasia are totally different things. I kind of wanted them to be connected, and they're not. When I asked Ms. Halvorsen, my speech therapist from middle school, whether I'd be "allowed" to be a speech therapist with a stutter, she said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet. I want to study speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics, or something to do with how the brain gets words back.

Last month the shop phone rang, and I said "Carvalho Shoe Service" on the third try. The guy on the other end waited through the first two. He wanted to know if we fix purses, which we don't.
