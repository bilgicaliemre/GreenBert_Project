In front of like sixty people, a lion ate a head of lettuce off a broom handle. That was my fault.

I have been the tail of a lion dance team since seventh grade. There are two people inside the lion, and the tail is the one bent over in the back holding the head's belt. Yes, the butt. Everybody says it. My best friend Bảo-Trân is the head and her dad, Chú Tâm, plays the drum. We practiced Saturdays at 7:30 in the morning in the back bay of his transmission shop on Wilcrest. I'm Mexican, my mom is from Zacatecas, so people always do a double take when the tail comes off and it's me.

Last February, for Tết, we had fourteen shows in three days. The second one was at a new bakery in the strip by the Dollar Tree. The owner taped the lettuce and the red envelope really high on the awning, which means a high stand: Bảo steps on my thighs and I lift her by the belt onto my shoulders. This time my left knee started shaking and I stepped back and she had to come down. The lion head hit the awning and the little mirror on the forehead popped off and rolled under a Camry. On the second try I couldn't even get her past my thighs. So the owner's son held the lettuce out on a broom, and the lion ate it off the broom. I literally let her down.

What I didn't tell anyone was why my knee shook. I had skipped six of the last eight Saturdays before Tết. I told Chú Tâm it was work, and it was, but I asked my coworker to trade shifts so it would be work. My legs weren't ready. That was on me.

After the bakery, Chú Tâm put Bảo's cousin Khôi in as tail for the rest of the weekend. He's fourteen and maybe 110 pounds. In the van I said, in front of everybody, "He's gonna fold like a lawn chair." He didn't. He got Bảo up on the first try that night and everybody screamed. I was on cymbals, which is what they give the little kids. Khôi did the other twelve shows.

It took me a long time to understand why I said that. I needed him to fold. If he folded, then the high stand was just hard and anybody would have messed it up. If he didn't, it was me. And he didn't.

For five years I told people I liked being the tail because it's chill and nobody sees your face. In December someone posted a TikTok from a grand opening we did, where the tail comes off and it's me, with the caption "the back half of the lion is in my AP Lang class" and a skull emoji. I said it was funny, whatever. But I checked it every day for a month. The first Saturday I skipped was nine days after it went up. Even at the bakery, when my knee was shaking, what I was actually thinking about was if anyone was filming. A step back looks like a decision. A fall just looks like a fall. Bảo could have landed on the curb.

This experience taught me that I didn't like being the tail because it was chill. I liked it because it was hiding, and those are not the same thing. When people could finally see me in it, I stopped showing up.

Chú Tâm wants an answer about this Tết by Thanksgiving. Khôi is the tail now, so I would be second tail or cymbals. I still don't know if I want to go back to fix last February or because I actually miss it. Last week I tried a wall sit. My record is 4:10 from sophomore year. I made it to 1:52. It's a start.
