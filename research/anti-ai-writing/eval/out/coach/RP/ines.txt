Until last month, I had never once answered the phone at my dad's shoe repair shop with its name. I said "Hi, shoe repair." The shop is Carvalho Shoe Service, on Pleasant Street in Fall River, and it has been there since 1994. Carvalho is my last name. It starts with a C.

I have a stutter, and it gets worse on the phone, especially on hard sounds like "C." So I swapped the name out. My dad noticed after about a year. He never said anything.

I work there every Saturday. I mostly do the counter and tickets, and since last summer I'm allowed to do heels and half-soles on the finishing machine. The shop smells like Barge cement and leather dust, and so does my hoodie. My friends say I smell like a "cowboy's basement." My dad's right thumb is stained black from the dye.

The shoes are the easy part. I can tell how someone walks from the wear on their heel. If the outer edge is worn down, they supinate. One lady's right heel was ground down way more than her left, and I said out loud that she must drive for work, because your right heel rests on the floor by the pedal. She did. She looked at me like I was a psychic. I keep a notebook of heels. It has 61 entries.

Last month the phone rang and I tried the name. It didn't come out. On the second try I almost hung up. I was trying to picture the guy's face, if he was annoyed. He just waited. On the third try I said "Carvalho Shoe Service." I didn't feel proud. I felt embarrassed that it took three tries. Then he asked if we fix purses. (We don't.) That was the best part, that it was nothing to him.

Since sophomore year I've kept a list on my phone of every lie I tell, even small ones like "I'm almost there." It has 212 items, mostly about being on time. I'm still not sure if the list makes me more honest or just better at noticing. "Hi, shoe repair" isn't on it. I think it was a lie too, a small one. I was swapping the true thing for an easier thing. I'm not sure what to do with that yet. Maybe add it to the list.

My avó, my grandmother Rosa, can't say my name either. Two years ago she had a stroke, and now she has aphasia. She understands more than she can say. Sometimes she calls me Lurdes, which was my aunt's name. Lurdes died before I was born. At first I corrected her. Now I don't.

I made her flashcards with pictures, in Portuguese, and we do them Tuesday and Thursday after school. She's relearning the words and I'm learning some of them for the first time. She's better than me at "agulha" (needle). I'm better at "telemóvel" (cell phone). When she gets a word wrong, I can't tell what she's thinking. Heels are easy compared to that. She can't say my name yet. She can say "sapato," which means shoe.

I read a lot about aphasia now, and I want to study how the brain gets words back. I found out stuttering and aphasia are totally different things. I kind of wanted them to be connected, and they're not. I asked Ms. Halvorsen, my speech therapist from middle school, if I'd be "allowed" to be a speech therapist with a stutter. She said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.

Once I asked my dad if he wanted me to take over the shop. He said, "The shop isn't a person, it doesn't need anyone." The first entry in my heel notebook is his work boots. I still don't know if that was a yes or a no.
