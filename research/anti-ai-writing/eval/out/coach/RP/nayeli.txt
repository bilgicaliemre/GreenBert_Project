In front of like sixty people, a lion ate a head of lettuce off a broom handle. That was my fault.

I have been the tail of a lion dance team since seventh grade. The tail is the back half, bent over, holding the head's belt. Yes, the butt. Everybody says it. My best friend Bảo-Trân is the head and her dad, Chú Tâm, plays the drum. I'm Mexican, my mom is from Zacatecas, so people always do a double take when the tail comes off and it's me.

Last February, for Tết, we had fourteen shows in three days. The second one was at a new bakery in the strip by the Dollar Tree. The owner taped the lettuce and red envelope high on the awning, which means a high stand: Bảo steps on my thighs and I lift her by the belt onto my shoulders. My left knee started shaking and I stepped back and she had to come down. The lion head hit the awning and the little mirror on the forehead popped off and rolled under a Camry. And I laughed. A nervous, stupid laugh. You hear everything in there, so Bảo heard it. On the second try I couldn't even get her past my thighs. So the owner's son held the lettuce out on a broom, and the lion ate it off the broom.

I never told anyone why my knee shook. I had skipped six of the last eight Saturday practices before Tết. I told Chú Tâm it was work, and it was, but I asked my coworker to trade shifts so it would be work. My legs weren't ready.

After the bakery, Chú Tâm put Bảo's cousin Khôi in as tail for the rest of the weekend. He's fourteen and maybe 110 pounds. In the van I said, in front of everybody, "He's gonna fold like a lawn chair." Bảo didn't look at me. She always looks at me in the van. I think she thought I was laughing at her. I did care. Maybe that's why I laughed? I don't know.

Khôi didn't fold. He got Bảo up on the first try that night and everybody screamed. I was on cymbals, which is what they give the little kids. Khôi did the other twelve shows. I never said sorry. I Venmo'd him ten dollars that said "for takis."

I needed him to fold. If he folded, the high stand was just hard. If he didn't, it was me. He didn't.

For five years I told people I liked being the tail because it's chill and nobody sees your face. In December, two months before Tết, someone posted a TikTok from a grand opening where the tail comes off and it's me, with the caption "the back half of the lion is in my AP Lang class" and a skull emoji. I said it was funny, whatever. But I checked it every day for a month. The first Saturday I skipped was nine days after it went up.

When my knee started shaking at the bakery, I was thinking about if anyone was filming. At Tết everybody's filming. If we fell, it would be on somebody's phone. A step back looks like a decision. I picked the one that looked better on video. Bảo could have landed on the curb.

I liked the tail because it was hiding, and that's not the same as chill. When people could finally see me in it, I stopped showing up.

Chú Tâm wants an answer about this Tết by Thanksgiving. Khôi is the tail now, so I would be second tail or cymbals. I don't know if I'd go back to fix last February or because I actually miss it. Bảo and I text every day, about everything except the laugh. My wall-sit record is 4:10, from sophomore year. Last week I made it to 1:52.
