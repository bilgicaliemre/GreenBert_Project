7. Lines that read like template writing

Three are covered above: the paragraph 3 topic switch (the essay announcing its own subject, plus a "not X, it is Y" turn), the "First... Second... Third..." list, and "being fast is not the same as being right."

Paragraph endings. Seven of your nine paragraphs end on a short punchy line of ten words or fewer:
"...nineteen minutes." / "He negotiated." / "...and the problem was me." / "He said 'dobra' and left." / "In April I deleted my comments." / "The electrical part is where I actually make mistakes." / "For a battery, being fast was exactly the problem."
One or two of these is great. Seven becomes a rhythm a reader notices, and it is the same rhythm heavily edited essays have. Keep the two you like most. I would keep "In April I deleted my comments" because it shows you changed something without stating a lesson. Let the other paragraphs end on their last fact, or join the short line to the sentence before it.

One line that looks like a template but isn't: "Not because I have 'golden hands', like my babcia says to everyone, but because..." Usually "not X but Y" is a warning sign. Here your babcia really says X, so it's fine, if you keep the EE sentence at all.

Grammar and clarity fixes

1. Paragraph 2: "since two years one corner is mine" -> "for two years one corner has been mine"
2. Paragraph 2: "Since summer 2024 I fixed 41 scooters" -> "Since summer 2024 I have fixed 41 scooters"
3. Paragraph 2: "In the basement of our staircase" -> "In the basement under our stairwell". In English "staircase" means the stairs themselves; for klatka, "stairwell" or "entrance" is closer.
4. Paragraph 4: "I replaced them with cells" -> "them" could mean the bad group or "the rest". Say which: "I replaced that group with cells".
5. Paragraph 4: "I thought it's fine, it's a mix" -> "I thought it was fine, it was a mix", or quote the thought: I thought, "It's fine, it's a mix." Quoting keeps your voice.
6. Paragraph 4: "I did not have a spot welder, they cost 400 złoty, so I soldered the strips." Comma splice -> "I did not have a spot welder (they cost 400 złoty), so I soldered the strips."
7. Paragraph 4: "three months warranty" -> "a three-month warranty"
8. Paragraph 5: "came down to the bike room almost at midnight" -> "came down to the bike room at almost midnight"
9. Paragraph 6: "When I opened it there was a little water at the bottom, there always is, it's Gdańsk, so I pointed at it." Four clauses joined by commas. Keep the words; put "there always is, it's Gdańsk" in parentheses, or end the sentence after "bottom".
10. Paragraph 6: "told him that is fair because of the water" -> "told him that was fair because of the water", or quote yourself directly.
11. Paragraph 7: "cooked, by me" is not a mistake. The comma is doing something; don't let a grammar checker remove it.
12. Paragraph 8: "the battery and the controller are exactly the part" -> "the parts" (two things).
13. Paragraph 8: "in one hour" -> "in an hour"
14. Paragraph 9: "my babcia" -> many American readers won't know the word. If you keep the line, either say "my grandma" or make sure the sentence shows who she is.
