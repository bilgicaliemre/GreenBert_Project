4. So what, and shape

After this draft, a reader knows you're observant, you stutter, you work at your dad's shop, you help your grandmother, and you want to study speech-language pathology. The details are specific, but the note a reader would write in the margin ("observant, caring, wants SLP") fits a lot of applicants.

Each one adds an interesting fact, but none changes what the paragraph before it meant, so the essay reads like a tour. Plan A puts events in cause order, so each paragraph sets up the next.

8. Lines that sound like essay-speak, not you

- "You can tell a lot about a person from their shoes." Anyone could write this, and the supination example already shows it. Cut.
- "But there is one part of the shop I have always avoided, and that is the phone." The "and that is" setup delays the point. It's also not quite accurate: you answered the phone for years. What you avoided was saying the name.
- "The other person who has taught me about words is my avó." There's no first person in the essay who taught you about words, and it's another "taught me."

The rest sounds like you talking: "after like a year," "cowboy's basement," "(We don't.)," "I kind of wanted." Leave those alone.

9. What works

- "My friends say I smell like a 'cowboy's basement.'" Funny, specific, and nobody else has it.
- "Then he just asked if we fix purses. (We don't.)" The parenthesis makes the moment as small as it was for him, which is your point.
- "She can't say my name yet, but she can say 'sapato,' which means shoe." The best paragraph ending in the draft. If your avó moves to a supplement, this line goes with her.
- "I still don't know if that was a yes or a no." Unresolved, and you leave it that way.

10. Small fixes (for lines that survive the revision)

1. "since last summer I'm allowed to do heels" → "since last summer I've been allowed to do heels" (tense).
2. "Last month I finally said it." "It" refers back to the name, but the sentence in between is about your dad, so readers may stumble. Name the thing.

5. "which was my aunt's name, who died before I was born" → "which was the name of my aunt, who died before I was born" ("who" was attaching to "name").
6. ", which I kind of wanted to be connected, and they're not." → ". I kind of wanted them to be connected, and they're not."
