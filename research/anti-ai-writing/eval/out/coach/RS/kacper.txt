Seven minutes and forty seconds. That is how long it takes me to change an inner tube on a Xiaomi M365, from wheel off to wheel on. My friend Szymon timed me. Then he timed a guy on YouTube: nineteen minutes.

I live in the falowiec in Przymorze, Gdańsk, a block of flats 850 meters long. In the basement under our stairwell there is a bike room, and for two years one corner has been mine: two shelves, a folding table from Lidl and one socket by the door. There I run KacperFix: I buy broken electric scooters on OLX, repair them and sell them. Since summer 2024 I have fixed 41 scooters, and I have 4.9 stars from 37 reviews. Mechanically you can understand a scooter in an hour. The battery and the controller are the parts I find most interesting, and also where I make mistakes.

In March a Glovo courier named Oleh brought me his Pro 2. The battery was dying on every hill, and he said, "I need it for work, it must be good." One group of ten cells was at 2.9 volts while the rest were almost 4. I replaced that group with used cells from another scooter, because new cells would eat my margin. I tested them and two had lower capacity, but I thought, "It's fine, it's a mix." I didn't have a spot welder (they cost 400 złoty), so I soldered the strips. I knew he needed it for work, and I put used cells in anyway. Oleh paid 380 złoty and I gave him a card I wrote by hand: "gwarancja 3 miesiące", a three-month warranty.

Nineteen days later, at 22:10, the scooter cut out on Grunwaldzka. It was raining and he still had an order in his bag. He pushed it four kilometers to my building and came down to the bike room at almost midnight, wet.

I would like to say my first thought was about him. It was about the 380 złoty. I said "it got wet" before I even unscrewed anything. When I opened it there was a little water at the bottom (there always is, it's Gdańsk), so I pointed at it. I gave him back 150 and told him that was fair because of the water. He said "dobra", okay, and walked out talking on the phone in Ukrainian, and I don't know what he said.

Two days later I opened the pack properly. One of my solder joints was cracked and the cell next to it was cooked, by me. In winter I argued for three days on a Facebook group that you can solder cells if you are fast enough. I was on that side. In April I deleted my comments. It was the repair, mine, not the rain, and it took me two days to say that, even just in my head.

A month later I bought a spot welder for 280 złoty, and a fire extinguisher for 89 złoty at Castorama, which now hangs next to the socket. And I stopped doing batteries for other people. It sounds like a lesson, but it is not really one, I just stopped doing the part where I can mess up. But batteries are exactly what I want to study in electrical engineering, so I don't know yet what that says about me.

The other part I didn't fix is Oleh. His number is still in my phone as "Oleh Pro2 Glovo". In June I saw him on Grunwaldzka on a new Ninebot, not one from me, and I didn't wave. He never left a review, and I still check sometimes. I have opened BLIK about four times to send him the other 230 złoty. I have the money. The problem is the message box, because I would have to write something in it, and I still don't know what.
