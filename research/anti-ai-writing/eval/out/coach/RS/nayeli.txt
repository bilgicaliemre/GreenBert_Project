In front of like sixty people, a lion ate a head of lettuce off a broom handle. That was my fault.

In seventh grade I became the tail on a Vietnamese lion dance team. Two people are inside the lion; the tail is bent over in back, holding the head's belt. Yes, the butt. My best friend Bảo-Trân is the head, and her dad, Chú Tâm, plays the drum. We practiced Saturday mornings in his transmission shop. I'm Mexican. My mom's from Zacatecas. So people do a double take when the tail comes off and it's me.

Last February, for Tết, the Vietnamese Lunar New Year, we had fourteen shows in three days. The second was a new bakery by the Dollar Tree. The lettuce and red envelope were taped high on the awning, which means a high stand: Bảo steps on my thighs and I lift her by the belt onto my shoulders. My left knee shook, I stepped back, and she had to come down. The head hit the awning, and the little forehead mirror popped off and rolled under a Camry. And I laughed, a nervous, stupid laugh. You hear everything inside the lion, so Bảo heard it. Second try, I couldn't get her past my thighs, so the owner's son held the lettuce out on a broom.

Bảo never brought up the laugh. But she didn't look at me in the van, and she always looks at me in the van. It sounds like I didn't care. I did. Maybe that's why I laughed. I don't know.

I never told anyone why my knee shook. I'd skipped six of the last eight Saturdays before Tết. I told Chú Tâm it was work, and it was, but I asked my coworker to trade shifts so it would be work. My legs weren't ready.

Chú Tâm put Bảo's cousin Khôi in as tail. He's fourteen and maybe 110 pounds. In the van I told everybody, "He's gonna fold like a lawn chair." He didn't. He got Bảo up first try that night and everybody screamed. I was on cymbals, which is what they give the little kids. Khôi did the other twelve shows.

I needed him to fold. If he folded, the high stand was just hard, and anybody would've messed it up. If he didn't, it was me.

For five years I told people I liked being the tail because it's chill and nobody sees your face. In December someone posted a TikTok of a grand opening where the tail comes off and it's me, caption "the back half of the lion is in my AP Lang class" with a skull emoji. I said it was funny, whatever. But I checked it every day for a month. 31,400 views. The first Saturday I skipped was nine days after it went up.

Back at the bakery, when my knee shook, I was thinking about whether anyone was filming. At Tết everybody films. If we fell, it would be on somebody's phone, so I stepped back before it could be a fall. A step back looks like a decision. I picked the one that looked better on video. Bảo could have landed on the curb.

I think I liked being the tail because it was hiding, which isn't the same as chill. The TikTok was the first time anybody actually saw me be the tail, and then I stopped going. I'm not saying that's why. I'm just saying the dates.

Chú Tâm wants an answer about next Tết by Thanksgiving. Khôi is the tail now, so I'd be second tail or cymbals. I still don't know if I'd go back to fix last February or because I actually miss it. Bảo and I text every day, about everything else. Last week I tried a wall sit. My record is 4:10 from sophomore year. I made it to 1:52.
