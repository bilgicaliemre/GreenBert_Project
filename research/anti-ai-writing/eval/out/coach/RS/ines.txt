Every Saturday I work the counter at my dad's shoe repair shop, Carvalho Shoe Service on Pleasant Street in Fall River. It smells like Barge cement and leather dust, and so does my hoodie. My friends say I smell like a "cowboy's basement." The part of the job I'm bad at is the phone. I have a stutter, and it gets worse on the phone, especially on hard sounds like "C." This is a problem when the shop is called Carvalho. So for years I picked up and said "Hi, shoe repair" instead of the name. My dad noticed after like a year and never said anything about it.

Sophomore year I got a 54 on my first AP Chem test. I told my mom it was an 85. I kept that up for three weeks, and when the progress report needed a parent signature, I signed it myself. Before I did, I practiced her signature about eight times on the back of a math worksheet. That's the part I would rather leave out, because it means I planned it. It wasn't a panic thing. Then my teacher, Mr. Oduya, emailed home. I didn't tell anyone this at the time, but I was relieved. Keeping the 85 straight for three weeks was exhausting.

My mom didn't yell. She just asked me to explain the test to her, question by question, at the kitchen table, in Portuguese, which I'm not good at for chemistry words. It took two hours. I didn't know the word for "mole," so I said "the unit thing, like a dozen." She laughed at that part. I ended up with a B+ in the class.

After that I made a rule. Every lie I tell goes on a list on my phone, even small ones like "I'm almost there." Since October of sophomore year it has 212 items. Most of them are about being on time. I'm still not sure if the list makes me more honest or just better at noticing.

Last month the shop phone rang and I answered it, and this time I tried to say the name. It didn't come out. I was trying to picture the guy's face on the other end, and I kept wondering if he was annoyed. On the second try I almost hung up. On the third try it came out: "Carvalho Shoe Service." He had just waited. I didn't feel proud. I mostly felt embarrassed that it took three tries. Then he asked if we fix purses. (We don't.) That was the best part, that it was nothing to him.

I didn't notice the next part on the call. I only noticed later, when someone asked me what I understand now that I didn't understand then. "Hi, shoe repair" was a lie too. A small one. It's not on my list. I had been doing the same thing with the phone that I did with the chemistry test: swapping the true thing for an easier thing. An 85 for a 54. "Shoe repair" for my own last name.

I can't leave my stutter out of this application, because it's the reason I spent years not saying my own last name. But I think the stutter was never the lie. The swap was. The stutter just made the easier thing easier. And the list that is supposed to catch all my lies, even "I'm almost there," never caught that one.

I'm not sure what to do with that yet. Maybe I'll add it to the list.
