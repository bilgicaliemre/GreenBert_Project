My grandmother, Rosa, can say "sapato" but not my name. Two years ago she had a stroke, and now she has aphasia: she understands more than she can say. On Tuesdays and Thursdays after school we go through picture flashcards I made for her in Portuguese. She's relearning words I'm learning for the first time. She beats me at "agulha," needle. I beat her at "telemóvel," cell phone.

That's where my question started: how does a brain get words back? I've been reading about aphasia ever since. I have a stutter, and I wanted stuttering and aphasia to be connected. They aren't. They're totally different things.

What pulls me in is figuring out what's going on with someone from the outside when they can't tell you. At my dad's shoe repair shop, I can tell how a person walks from the wear on their heels. One woman's right heel was ground down far more than her left; it turned out she drives for work. I keep a notebook of heels with 61 entries. Heels are easy. My avó isn't. When she gets a word wrong, I can't tell what she's thinking. I'm not sure the heels and my grandmother are really connected, or if I just want them to be.

I want to study speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics. My middle school speech therapist told me some of the best speech therapists stutter. I don't fully believe her yet. I'd like to find out.
