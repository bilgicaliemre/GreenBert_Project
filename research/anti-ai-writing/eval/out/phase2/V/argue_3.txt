I don't think every member of a group project should get the same grade. A grade is supposed to show what one student learned and what that student did. When four people get the same score on one slideshow, the grade describes the slideshow and nothing else. Teachers can still give the finished project one score, but a real share of each person's grade should come from their own work.

Under a shared grade, the work usually lands on whoever cares most about the grade. Say a group of four has two weeks to make a presentation. If one person needs an A to keep their average up and another doesn't care much, both of them know who's going to give in first. It's usually the one who cares, and it usually happens the night before it's due, when the shared slides are still half empty and the group chat has gone quiet. That person finishes everything, and the one who did nothing gets the same A. Nobody has to plan to be lazy for this to happen. Waiting is just a smart move, as long as somebody else in the group is more worried than you are.

A shared grade also hides what each person learned. If a lab group gets a 92 on a report about enzymes, all four students get a 92 in the gradebook. Maybe one of them ran the calculations and could explain every graph, and another only typed up the materials list. The gradebook can't tell them apart. The unit test usually can, since the student who skipped the work also skipped the practice, and the 92 never warned that student or the teacher.

The best argument for one grade is that jobs work this way. When a team at a company misses a deadline, the whole team looks bad, so students might as well get used to it now. But at a job, a manager sees who does what over months, and someone who never contributes can be moved off the team or fired. In school, a teacher usually sees a group work for a few class periods and then gets one finished file. Students can't fire a groupmate, and often they don't even get to choose one.

Peer review forms, the usual way to split grades, don't work well on their own. Friends tend to give each other full marks, and most people don't want to be the one who reports a classmate. A simpler setup is to put each member in charge of one section with their name on it (if the project is a shared Google Doc, the teacher can also check the edit history). Then each person answers a couple of questions about the whole project, out loud or on paper. That's the fairest check I can think of, because someone who did the work can answer them and someone who didn't usually can't.

Separate grades won't make group projects fun. Groups will still argue, and some students will still do as little as they can get away with. The student who cares most, though, would lose the main reason to do the whole project alone. Right now, anyone else's slow start costs that student points. If part of each grade came from each person's own section, that student could let the others do their parts, and the others would actually have to learn the material in those sections.
