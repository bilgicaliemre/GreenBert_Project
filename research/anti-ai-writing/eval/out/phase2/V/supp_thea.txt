Dear roommate,

My brother Lukas, who's 13, calls me "the Grim Reaper's DJ," so you may as well hear it from me. I'm the organist at Immanuel Lutheran in Hartington, Nebraska, and the funeral home calls me too, so a lot of my Saturdays are a funeral at 10:30, then the lunch in the basement.

I play the pedals in socks because my shoes slip. Once at a funeral lunch a little kid pointed at me and said, "That lady has cats on her feet." Which, yes. I keep count: 48 funerals since June 2024, "How Great Thou Art" 23 times. I kind of hate that hymn, and it's the one I play best. Before Duane, the funeral director, meets a family, we each guess which hymn they'll pick, and I'm up 19 to 14.

We're not even Lutheran. When I told Pastor Jill I'm not sure I believe any of it, she said that's allowed, and I still read the readings ahead of time.

I don't get sad at funerals; it's a job. I've never cried at one. Okay, once, at Gerald Kreikemeier's. He'd had Husker season tickets since 1971, and his family wanted "There Is No Place Like Nebraska" for the postlude, so I learned it off YouTube in two days. But that was the song. It doesn't count. For someone who counts everything, I'm strict about leaving that one out.

I've played 48 funerals, and I've never once been to one as just a guest.
