I think students should be allowed to retake major tests, and if they score higher the second time, the new score should replace the old one. A test is supposed to measure what a student knows. If I fail a chemistry test on stoichiometry in October and finally understand it by November, my grade should show that I understand it. Without a retake, it shows that I failed, and it keeps showing that until the semester ends.

Low scores don't always mean a student didn't learn the material. Sometimes it was just a bad morning. Maybe you were getting sick, or you had a history test and a chemistry test on the same day and only had time to study for one of them. Major tests can count for a big part of a quarter grade, so one bad day can drag down months of work.

Retakes also change what students do after a test comes back. In a class with no retakes, most people check the score and maybe the questions they missed, and then the test goes into a folder and stays there. The unit is over. The grade can't change, so there's no reason to go back. That's a problem in subjects like math and chemistry, where each unit builds on the one before it, and anyone who never figured out unit three walks into unit four already behind. When a retake is possible, the same student has a reason to sit down with every question they got wrong and work through it.

The strongest argument against retakes is that students will stop trying on the first attempt. If you can always take it again, why study the first time? I think that's a real risk, which is why retakes should come with conditions. To earn one, a student should have to correct every question they missed and explain in writing why the right answer is right. Then they take a different version of the test, once, within a week or two. All of that is more work than studying properly the first time, so most students would still take the first test seriously. Allowing only one retake per test also limits how many extra versions a teacher has to write and grade.

Another objection is fairness. Say a student studied hard and got an A, and the person in the next seat got a C, did the corrections, and raised it to a B on the retake. The first student might feel cheated, but their A didn't shrink. Unless the class is graded on a curve, one student's grade doesn't take anything away from another's.

You also hear that the real world doesn't give second chances. It gives a lot of them. If you fail your driving test, you can take it again, and you can take the SAT more than once. Nobody thinks a license is worth less because the driver passed on the second try; what people care about is whether the person can drive.

I wouldn't extend this to final exams, though. After a final there's no next unit and no time left in the semester to relearn anything, so most of the reasons for a retake disappear. During the year it's different. A grade that can still change gives students a reason to keep working on material after the test date, and a grade that can't tells them to move on whether they understand it or not.
