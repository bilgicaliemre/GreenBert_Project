Phones away from bell to bell

I think high schools should ban student phones for the whole school day, from the first bell to the last, including lunch and the time between classes. A lot of students would hate this rule, and I get why. If a few hours without a phone sounds that hard, that says something about how much of our day phones already take up.

Many schools already tell students to keep phones away during class, but that rule has to be enforced again in every class. When a teacher tells everyone to put their phones away, some go face down on the desk and some slide into a lap under it. Then the teacher can either let it go or stop the lesson to argue with one student while everyone else waits. If phones were locked up each morning, there'd be nothing to argue about. Locking phones up also beats leaving them in backpacks. Even a zipped-up phone pulls at you, because when it buzzes during a lecture, part of your attention leaves the room to guess who texted.

Lunch and the hallways should be part of the ban, even though that's the part students will fight hardest. At a lot of lunch tables, people sit next to each other and look at separate screens. When someone trips or a fight breaks out, phones often come out to film it before a teacher even gets there. Without phones, lunch is the one part of the day when you have to talk to the person across from you. That can be awkward, but it's also how you end up friends with someone who isn't already in your group chat.

The best argument against a ban is safety. Parents want to reach their kids in an emergency, and with school shootings in the news, that worry makes sense to me. But during a lockdown, students are supposed to stay silent and follow the teacher's directions, and a ringing phone could give away where a class is hiding. On a normal day, parents can call the office, the way they did before everyone had a cell phone. The phones would sit in a pouch or a locker until the last bell, and a student with a medical need (for example, a diabetes monitor that runs through a phone app) could get an exception.

Some teachers use phones in class, for online quizzes or to take a picture of the board, but a school laptop or paper can cover most of that. The harder objection is that teenagers need to learn to manage their phones on their own, since adults have to. I agree that it's a skill. I just don't think the place to practice it is the middle of a chemistry lesson. People learn to drive with a permit and an adult in the passenger seat before they're allowed on the highway alone, and phone habits could be learned the same way, at home and on weekends, while the school day stays phone-free.

The ban won't fix everything, and the group chat will still be waiting after school. It would give students their attention back while they're in the building and give teachers back the minutes they lose fighting over phones. That only works if the rule is enforced the same way for everyone. If teachers let some students keep a phone on the desk while everyone else's is locked up, students will stop taking the rule seriously, and the old arguments will start again in every class.
