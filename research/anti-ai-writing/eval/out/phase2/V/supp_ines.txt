I keep a notebook of heels. It has 61 entries, starting with my dad's work boots. From the counter at his shop, Carvalho Shoe Service, I can tell how someone walks by the wear on their heels. One woman's right heel was ground down far more than her left, because she drives for work and that heel rests on the floor by the pedal. When I said so, she looked at me like I was a psychic.

My avó, my grandmother Rosa, is harder to read. She had a stroke two years ago and has aphasia: she understands more than she can say. Tuesdays and Thursdays we do picture flashcards I made in Portuguese. She's relearning the words and I'm learning them for the first time; she's better at "agulha" (needle) and I'm better at "telemóvel" (cell phone). She can say "sapato," shoe, but not my name yet. I can't tell what she's thinking when she gets a word wrong.

I read a lot about aphasia now, because I want to know how a brain gets words back. I have a stutter, worse on hard sounds like the C in Carvalho, and I kind of wanted the two to be connected. I found out they're totally different things.

Heels and my avó are both about figuring out what's going on with someone from the outside, when they can't tell you. I don't know if that's a real connection or one I just want, like the stutter and the aphasia.
