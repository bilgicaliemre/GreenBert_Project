Until February, my Saturdays started with lion dance at 7:30 a.m., under two cars on the lifts in Chú Tâm's transmission shop. My best friend Bảo-Trân was the head, and I was the tail. I held her belt and couldn't see anything but her feet and the floor, so I followed her feet.

For five years I called the tail chill because nobody sees your face. In December someone posted a TikTok where the tail comes off and it's me, captioned "the back half of the lion is in my AP Lang class." I checked it every day for about a month: 31,400 views. Nine days after it went up, I started skipping Saturdays, six of the last eight before Tết, the Lunar New Year. I'm not saying that's why. I'm just saying the dates.

At a bakery that Tết, the lion's lettuce hung high, so Bảo stepped onto my thighs for the high stand. My left knee started shaking, and my first thought was whether anyone was filming. I stepped back before it could be a fall. A step back looks like a decision; I picked the one that looked better on video. Bảo could have landed on the curb.

I think I liked the tail because it's hiding. That's not the same as chill. I still sleep in the furry tail pants. Chú Tâm wants an answer by Thanksgiving, and I can't tell if I'd go back to fix last February or because I actually miss it.
