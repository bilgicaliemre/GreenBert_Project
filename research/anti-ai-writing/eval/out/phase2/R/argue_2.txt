Let Students Retake Tests

I think schools should let students retake major tests to raise their grade, as long as students have to earn the retake. A grade in chemistry is supposed to show how well you understand chemistry. Sometimes a low score comes from misreading the directions or a bad night at home, and sometimes the student just hadn't learned the material yet when the test came. Say a student fails the stoichiometry unit test, then works through every problem they missed and keeps asking the teacher questions until it makes sense. If the F stays, the report card says that student doesn't understand stoichiometry, and that's no longer true.

Retakes also change what students do with a bad test. When there's no second chance, a failed test is over. There's no reason to study material that's already been graded, so a lot of students look at the score and put the paper away. If a retake is possible, that same test becomes a list of what you need to learn before the next try. Now you have a reason to go through the wrong answers and ask about the ones that still don't make sense. In a class like math, where each unit builds on the one before it, a student who never goes back to fix the first unit will have a harder time with the next one.

The obvious objection is that retakes make students lazy. If you know you can try again, why study the first time? That's a real risk if retakes are free and unlimited, so they shouldn't be. A teacher could require test corrections or a review session before anyone retakes. The retake score could be capped (or averaged with the first score), and each student could get only one or two retakes a semester. Under rules like that, someone who skips studying ends up doing corrections and taking a second test for a score that's capped or averaged down anyway, which is more work than studying the first time.

You also hear that the real world doesn't give second chances, so school shouldn't either. That's only partly true. People who fail the driving test take it again, and once they pass, their license is the same as everyone else's. Professional licensing exams can be retaken too, and writers revise their drafts before anyone publishes them. In a lot of adult life, what counts is whether you eventually get it right. When adults fail at something, they're expected to figure out what went wrong and fix it, which is exactly what a retake asks a student to do.

Teachers have a fair complaint too: retakes mean writing new versions of tests and finding time to give them. Shared question banks and set retake days would help, but they don't make the work go away. I think it's worth it anyway, because a student who fails one major test with no way to recover has less reason to keep trying in that class for the rest of the semester.

The first test would still count under these rules. It decides whether you need a retake at all, and if the scores are averaged, it stays part of the grade. The retake covers the same material, so the standard doesn't drop. What changes is the deadline. A student who didn't understand stoichiometry on test day gets until the retake to learn it, and has to prove it on a new test to get the credit.
