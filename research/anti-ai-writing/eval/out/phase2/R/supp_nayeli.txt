From seventh grade until February, I was the tail in Chú Tâm's lion dance troupe. My best friend, Bảo-Trân, is the head. Holding her belt, I saw only her feet and the floor, so I followed her feet. I always said I liked the tail because nobody sees your face.

Someone posted a TikTok from a December grand opening: the tail comes off, and it's me. I checked it daily for a month (31,400 views). Nine days after it went up, I skipped my first Saturday. I'm not saying that's why. By Tết, the Lunar New Year, I'd missed six of the last eight.

At a bakery during Tết, we tried the high stand: Bảo steps onto my thighs and I lift her by the belt onto my shoulders. When my left knee shook, my first thought was whether anyone was filming. A step back looks like a decision, so I stepped back before it became a fall, not knowing if Bảo would land on the curb.

Her cousin Khôi, fourteen, replaced me, and in the van I said he'd fold like a lawn chair. He got her up first try while I played cymbals. I needed him to fold. If he folded, the tail was just hard; if he didn't, it was me.

I think I liked the tail because it's hiding. Chú Tâm wants an answer by Thanksgiving, and I can't tell if I'd go back to fix last February or because I actually miss it.
