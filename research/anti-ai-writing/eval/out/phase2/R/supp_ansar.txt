I've tagged 37 wild apple trees in the foothills past Talgar: Malus sieversii, the wild ancestor of basically every apple. Wild ones run from about 9 to 15 Brix, the percent sugar in the juice, and Tree 23, my best, read 15.2.

I took a bag to Zhanna Serikovna, a researcher at the botanical garden. She cut one open, looked for ten seconds and said, "This one has been to the city." The tree is 30 meters from a picnic spot, probably a hybrid with a dacha garden apple. I wanted to argue. I had 15.2 in my notebook, as if that proves where a tree comes from. On the bus home I ate three, and they were really good. I was angry at her and at the apples, which makes no sense.

I got defensive for a tree. I think it's because my grandmother jokes that I'm shala-kazakh, half-Kazakh, since I answer her in Russian. Tree 23 is a city one, half one thing and half the other.

The wild forests are shrinking, though I don't fully trust the "70 percent gone since the 1960s" that every website copies. I want to study plant or conservation genetics and be the person who decides which trees get saved, whatever that job is called.

Zhanna Serikovna said some places cut out hybrids so they don't pollinate the wild trees. If Tree 23 were my decision, I don't know if I'd cut it. Probably the correct answer is yes.
