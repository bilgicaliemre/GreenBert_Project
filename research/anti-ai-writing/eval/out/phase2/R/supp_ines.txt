I made Portuguese picture flashcards for my avó, my grandmother Rosa, and we do them Tuesdays and Thursdays. Two years ago she had a stroke, and now she has aphasia: she understands more than she can say. It's funny, because she's relearning words I'm learning for the first time. She's better than me at "agulha" (needle), and I'm better at "telemóvel" (cell phone). She can't say my name yet, but she can say "sapato," shoe.

I read a lot about aphasia now, because I want to know how a brain gets words back. I kind of wanted aphasia to be connected to my stutter, but they're totally different things.

At my dad's shoe repair shop I read heels; my heel notebook has 61 entries. One woman's right heel was ground down much more than her left; she drives for work, so that heel rests on the floor by the pedal. Heels and avó both mean figuring out what's going on with someone from the outside, when they can't tell you.

Heels are easy. When avó gets a word wrong, I can't tell what she's thinking. I don't know if that's a real connection or if I just want it to be one, like the stutter and the aphasia.

I want to study speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics. When I asked my middle school speech therapist, Ms. Halvorsen, whether I'd be "allowed" to be one with a stutter, she said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.
