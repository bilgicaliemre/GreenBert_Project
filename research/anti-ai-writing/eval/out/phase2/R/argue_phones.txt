Phones should stay out of the school day

When a phone buzzes in the middle of class, it's rarely just the owner who reacts. Half the class reaches for their pockets, the teacher stops mid-sentence, and by the time everyone settles, a few people are scrolling under their desks. I think high schools should ban student phones from the first bell to the last, with exceptions for medical needs. Phones cost students focus in class and conversation at lunch, and the usual alternative, trusting each student to resist on their own, puts the whole job on willpower plenty of adults don't have either.

Learning is the most obvious problem. Hard work, like balancing a chemistry equation or getting through a dense page of Shakespeare, needs attention that lasts. A phone in your pocket pulls at that attention even when it's silent, because part of your mind keeps wondering what you're missing. Checking a notification "for a second" also takes longer than a second, since afterward you have to find your place in the problem again. Over a whole period, those restarts eat a real piece of the lesson. Teachers lose time too. Every minute spent telling someone to put a phone away is a minute of teaching the whole class loses, including the students who never took theirs out.

Phones also cost students something outside class. Lunch and passing periods are when students talk, joke, argue and meet people outside their usual group. In a cafeteria where phones are allowed, though, you'll often see a table of friends sitting together, each looking at a different screen. Those unplanned minutes are where friendships start, especially for students who are new or shy, and it's hard to start a conversation with someone whose eyes are on a phone. Drama comes in with the phones, too: a cruel group chat or an embarrassing video can spread through a whole grade before the next period begins. Taking phones away during the day won't end online fights, but it gives students several hours away from them.

The other option is to teach students to use phones responsibly. That sounds reasonable, but it asks teenagers to win a fight that many adults lose. Apps are built to keep people scrolling. Expecting a sixteen-year-old to ignore one during a slow lecture is like putting a plate of cookies on every desk and grading students on not eating them. A clear rule takes the decision out of the moment, and it applies to everyone at once. If nobody has a phone, nobody is left out of the conversation happening online, and nobody has to be the one person who puts theirs away while the rest of the class keeps going.

The strongest objection is safety. Parents want to reach their kids in an emergency, and that's fair. But schools already have office phones and ways to contact families quickly. In a real crisis, hundreds of students calling and texting at once could spread panic and rumors and drown out instructions from the adults in charge. Academic uses like calculators or research can be handled with laptops or tablets meant for schoolwork, and students who use a phone to monitor a health condition should get an exception.

The ban I'm arguing for is limited. It covers the school day and nothing else, and students have their phones again at the final bell. So deciding on a ban is really deciding what those hours are for, and I think they're for the lesson in front of you and the people sitting next to you.
