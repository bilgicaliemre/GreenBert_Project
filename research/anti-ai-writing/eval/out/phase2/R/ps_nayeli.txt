Lion dance practice was 7:30 on Saturday mornings in the back bay of Chú Tâm's transmission shop, under two cars up on the lifts. Chú Tâm played the drum, his daughter Bảo-Trân (my best friend) was the head, and I was the tail, holding her belt and seeing only her feet and the floor. I got the spot in seventh grade because nobody else could squat with Bảo, all 4'11" of her, standing on my legs. For five years I've said I like being the tail because it's chill and nobody sees your face.

This February, at Tết, the Lunar New Year, we did fourteen shows in three days. At the second, a new bakery, the lettuce and red envelope were taped high on the awning, so we did the high stand: Bảo steps on my thighs, and I lift her by the belt onto my shoulders. My left knee started shaking. I stepped back, and she had to come down. The lion head hit the awning, and its forehead mirror popped off and rolled under a Camry. On the second try I couldn't get her past my thighs, so the owner's son held the lettuce out on a broom handle, and the lion ate it off the broom in front of about sixty people.

When the mirror rolled away, I laughed, a nervous, stupid laugh, and inside the lion you hear everything. It sounds like I didn't care. I did, and maybe that's why I laughed; I don't know. Bảo thought I was laughing at her. She didn't say so, but in the van she didn't look at me, and she always looks at me in the van.

Chú Tâm put in Bảo's cousin Khôi, fourteen and maybe 110 pounds. In the van, in front of everybody, I said, "He's gonna fold like a lawn chair." That night he got Bảo up on the first try, everybody screamed, and I was on cymbals, which is what they give the little kids. I never said sorry; I Venmo'd him ten dollars with the note "for takis," and I guess that was the sorry.

My knee shook because I'd skipped six of the last eight Saturdays before Tết. I told Chú Tâm it was work, and it was, because I'd asked Itzel to trade shifts so it would be. In December someone had posted a clip where the tail comes off and it's me, captioned "the back half of the lion is in my AP Lang class." I said it was funny, whatever, then checked it every day for about a month. It got 31,400 views. I'm not saying that's why, but the first Saturday I skipped was nine days after it went up.

With Bảo on my thighs and my knee shaking, I was thinking about whether anyone was filming. I stepped back before it could be a fall on somebody's phone. A step back looks like a decision, and I picked the one that would look better on video. Bảo could have landed on the curb, and she didn't, but I didn't know that when I stepped back.

I think I liked the tail because it's hiding, which isn't the same as chill. The TikTok was the first time anybody actually saw me be the tail, and then I stopped going. I needed Khôi to fold: if he folded, the position was just hard, and if he didn't, it was me.

Tết is February 6th, and Chú Tâm wants an answer by Thanksgiving. Khôi is the tail now, so I'd be second tail or on cymbals, and I can't tell if I'd go back to fix last February or because I actually miss it. Bảo and I text every day, about everything except the laugh. I still have the furry tail pants. Chú Tâm never asked for them back, and I sleep in them.
