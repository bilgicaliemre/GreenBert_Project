Same project, different grades

A slideshow is due in two days. Half the slides are still blank, and one person in the group hasn't answered a text since the project was assigned. Somebody, usually whoever cares most about their grade, stays up late making that person's slides. A week later the whole group gets a B-plus, so the student who did the extra work and the student who disappeared end up with exactly the same grade. That isn't fair. Group members shouldn't automatically get the same grade, and I think teachers should grade each student at least partly on what that student actually contributed.

The best argument for giving everyone the same grade is that it teaches teamwork. At a job, the argument goes, a team succeeds or fails together, so students should learn to depend on each other and take responsibility for the whole group. I agree that a shared stake can push a group to talk, divide up the tasks and check each other's work. The grade itself, though, only measures the final product. A group can earn an A because one strong student did everything while the rest watched, and that A tells the teacher nothing about whether anyone learned to cooperate. The job comparison doesn't help much either, since a coworker who never does their work eventually faces consequences, and a student who skips their part of a group project faces none.

A grade on a report card is supposed to describe what one student knows and can do. If the student who wrote nothing gets the same score as the student who wrote the whole paper, the grade is wrong about both of them: it's too high for one, and it hides the other one's work. Over a semester with several group projects, those errors add up, and part of the final grade ends up measuring luck (who you happened to get grouped with).

Identical grades also change how students act, because students are practical about effort. If the grade will be the same no matter what, the easy move for someone with a busy week is to let the most anxious person in the group pick up the slack. The student who picks it up learns something too: doing more than your share is expected, and nobody rewards it. Neither of those is what the teacher meant to teach. After enough projects like that, students start to dread group work, which defeats the whole point of assigning it.

A fairer system would mix shared and individual grading. Part of the grade can come from the final product, which everyone shares. The rest can come from evidence about each person: a short reflection on what they did, a confidential peer evaluation, or a quick check-in where the teacher asks each member to explain a section of the work. None of these is perfect. Peer evaluations can be swayed by friendships, and people can exaggerate in reflections. Still, any of them gives the teacher more information than one score for the whole group, and they give every student a reason to do their part.

Group projects are still worth assigning. Planning a project with other people and working through disagreements are real skills. But when every member gets the same grade no matter what, the grade that was supposed to teach teamwork tells students that individual effort doesn't matter, and many of them act on that.
