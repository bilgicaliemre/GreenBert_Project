I've tagged 37 wild apple trees in the foothills past Talgar, and Tree 23 was my best: 15.2 Brix (percent sugar in the juice). I took a bag to Zhanna Serikovna, a botanical garden researcher. She cut one open, looked for ten seconds and said, "This one has been to the city." Tree 23 grows 30 meters from a picnic spot and is probably a hybrid with some dacha apple.

I wanted to argue. I had 15.2 in my notebook, as if a number proves where a tree comes from. On the bus home I ate three. They were really good, and I was angry at her and also at the apples.

The wild trees are Malus sieversii, the ancestor of basically every apple. Some places, she told me, cut out hybrids so they don't pollinate wild trees. I want to be the person who decides which trees get saved; I don't know what that job is called. Websites say 70 percent of the forests are gone since the 1960s, but they all copy one sentence, so I don't fully trust it.

I think Tree 23 bothered me because my äje (grandmother) calls me "shala-kazakh," half-Kazakh, since I answer her in Russian. Here was a city tree, half one thing, half the other, and I got defensive for it. That might be a bit much, but I think it's true. If Tree 23 were my decision, I don't know if I'd cut it. Probably the correct answer is yes.
