Tuesdays and Thursdays after school, my avó Rosa and I go through the Portuguese picture flashcards I made her. She had a stroke two years ago and has aphasia. She understands more than she can say. She can say "sapato," but not my name yet. The funny part is she's relearning words I'm learning for the first time: she's better than me at "agulha" (needle), and I'm better at "telemóvel" (cell phone).

How does a brain get words back? I read a lot about aphasia now, and I found out it's totally different from stuttering. I stutter, worst on hard sounds like the C in Carvalho, my last name, and I'd kind of wanted the two to be connected.

At my dad's shoe repair shop, I can read how someone walks from their heels: a worn outer edge means they supinate. My notebook of heels has 61 entries. Heels and my avó both mean figuring out what's going on with someone from the outside, when they can't tell you. With heels it's easy. With her it isn't: when she gets a word wrong, I can't tell what she's thinking. I don't know if that connection is real or one I just want, like the stutter and the aphasia.

I want to study speech-language pathology. When I asked Ms. Halvorsen, my middle school speech therapist, if I'd be "allowed" to be one with a stutter, she said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.
