For five years I was the tail in Chú Tâm's lion dance, practicing Saturdays at 7:30 in his transmission shop under two cars on the lifts. His daughter Bảo-Trân, my best friend, was the head; I held her belt and saw only her feet. I said I liked the tail because nobody sees your face.

Someone posted a TikTok from a December grand opening: the tail comes off and it's me, captioned "the back half of the lion is in my AP Lang class." It got 31,400 views. Nine days after it went up, I skipped my first Saturday. I'm not saying that's why. I'm just saying the dates.

By Tết I'd skipped six of the last eight, so at Phượng Hoàng Bakery, with Bảo on my thighs, my left knee shook. My first thought was whether anyone was filming. A step back looks like a decision, so I stepped back, and Bảo could have landed on the curb.

Her cousin Khôi, fourteen, replaced me. In the van I said, "He's gonna fold like a lawn chair." He got her up first try. I needed him to fold: if he folds, the position is just hard; if he doesn't, it's me. I think I liked the tail because it's hiding. Chú Tâm wants an answer by Thanksgiving. I can't tell if I'd go back to fix last February or because I miss it. He never asked for the furry tail pants back. I sleep in them.
