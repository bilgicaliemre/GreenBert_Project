Let Us Try Again

Every student knows the feeling. You get a test back, flip it over, and see a number that does not match what you thought you knew. Maybe you studied the wrong chapter. Maybe you were sick, had three other tests that week, or just froze. Whatever happened, that grade usually sits in the gradebook for the rest of the semester. I think that is a mistake. Students should be allowed to retake major tests to raise their grade, as long as they do real work to earn the second try.

The main reason is simple. A grade is supposed to show what a student knows. Say a student fails a chemistry test on balancing equations in October, then goes back, practices, and can balance any equation you hand her by November. Which grade describes her better? The October score describes one bad afternoon. The November score describes what she can actually do. When a school refuses a retake, it chooses to record a student's worst day instead of her current skill, and that makes the grade less honest.

Retakes also change what students do after a test. Right now, when most of us bomb an exam, we look at the score, feel bad for a few minutes, and shove the paper into the bottom of a backpack. We never look at the mistakes again because there's no reason to. A retake gives us a reason to go back. Suddenly the wrong answers matter. If a student has to correct every missed question and meet with the teacher before retesting, she ends up learning the material she skipped the first time. Isn't that the whole point of school?

There is also a fairness problem. Students don't walk into a test with the same week behind them. Some have quiet homes and parents who can pay for tutors. Others work evening shifts or watch younger siblings until midnight. One rough test can pull a semester grade down a full letter, and that letter goes on a transcript that colleges read. A kid whose family was falling apart for a week shouldn't carry that mark forever if she can prove she knows the content.

The usual objection is that retakes make students lazy. If you know you get another shot, why study for the first one? I understand the worry, and I've watched classmates treat a first attempt like a practice round. But that problem has easy fixes. Teachers can require test corrections and a review session before anyone retakes. They can cap the retake score at a B, or average the two attempts. They can allow one retake per unit instead of unlimited tries. With rules like these, blowing off the first test just creates more work, and most students figure that out fast.

Another objection is that real life doesn't hand out second chances. Actually, it hands them out all the time. People retake the driving test. Workers redo a report when a boss sends it back. Athletes lose a game and play the same team again next season. What adults rarely face is a permanent penalty for not understanding something on the first try, with no way to show they've learned it since.

School should be the place where failing once is allowed, as long as you fix it. A retake policy with real requirements tells students that effort after a mistake counts. A policy with no retakes tells them that one bad Tuesday is the final word. I know which message I'd rather hear the next time I flip a test over.
