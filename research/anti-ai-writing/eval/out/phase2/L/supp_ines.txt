My avó, Rosa, can say "sapato." She can't say my name yet. A stroke two years ago left her with aphasia. She understands more than she can say. Tuesdays and Thursdays we do picture flashcards I made for her in Portuguese. She's relearning words I'm learning for the first time. She's better at "agulha," needle. I'm better at "telemóvel."

That's where my question started. How does the brain get words back? I read a lot about aphasia now. I found out stuttering and aphasia are totally different things. I have a stutter, so I kind of wanted them to be connected. They're not.

What keeps me reading is the guessing. At my dad's shoe repair shop I can tell how someone walks from the wear on their heel. An outer edge worn down means they supinate. One woman's right heel was ground down way more than her left, and it turned out she drives for work. My notebook of heels has 61 entries. Heels are easy, though. When my avó gets a word wrong, I can't tell what she's thinking. Sometimes she calls me Lurdes, my aunt's name. My aunt died before I was born. I used to correct her. Now I don't.

Both are about figuring out what's going on with someone from the outside, when they can't tell you. I don't know yet if that connection is real or just one I want to be there. Speech-language pathology seems like the place to find out.
