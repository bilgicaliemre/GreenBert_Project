One Project, Four Grades

Every group project I've been part of has had the same cast. One person makes a shared doc on the first night and starts typing. One or two people show up when reminded. And one person sends a single message the night before it's due: "what do u need me to do." Then all four of us get a B+.

That system is unfair, and teachers should stop using it. A grade is supposed to tell you, and anyone who reads your transcript, what you did. When four people get one letter for unequal work, the letter stops meaning anything for at least three of them.

The usual defense of a shared grade goes like this. In the real world, teams succeed or fail together. If a company's product flops, nobody hands out separate scores. Teachers want students to learn that their success depends on other people, and a shared grade pushes them to care about the whole project instead of just their own slice.

I get the idea. It just doesn't match what happens in a classroom. At a job, a manager can usually tell who isn't pulling their weight, and that person eventually pays for it. They get passed over, or they get let go. In a group project, the student who does nothing pays nothing. They walk away with the same grade as the person who stayed up rewriting the conclusion. If a shared grade teaches anything, it teaches the hard worker that effort is optional for everyone else, and it teaches the slacker that someone will always cover for them.

It also traps the responsible students. When a teammate won't do their part, you have two choices. You can do the missing work yourself, or you can let the project sink and take the hit with everyone else. Most people pick the first option because they care about their grade. So the shared grade ends up rewarding whoever does the least, since that person can count on a more anxious classmate stepping in. That's backwards.

Some people worry that individual grades would make group work pointless. Students would split the project into four pieces and never talk to each other. That's a fair worry, but there's a middle path. A teacher can give part of the grade for the final product and part for each person's own contribution. Measuring contribution isn't that hard. Shared documents keep a version history that shows who wrote what. Students can fill out short, private evaluations of their teammates. A teacher can ask each person to explain one section out loud. None of these methods is perfect, but any of them beats pretending everyone did the same amount.

A split grade keeps what's good about group work. Students still have a reason to make the final product strong, because part of their score rides on it. They still have to plan together, compromise, and deal with the friend who insists on Comic Sans. What changes is that the kid who disappears for two weeks can't expect a free ride.

Group projects should stay. Working with other people is a real skill, and school is a decent place to practice it. But a grade should reflect the work a person actually did. When teachers hand everyone the same score no matter what, they punish the students who show up and reward the ones who don't. Grading fairly takes a little more effort from the teacher. Given how many students have carried a group on their backs, I think it's worth it.
