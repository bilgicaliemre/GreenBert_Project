Dear Roommate,

Back home in Hartington, Nebraska, my Saturdays depend on whether somebody died. I'm the organist at Immanuel Lutheran, and the funeral home calls me too. I've played 48 funerals since June 2024. I keep track.

I play the organ pedals in socks because my shoes slip. At one funeral lunch a little kid pointed at me and announced, "That lady has cats on her feet." Which, yes.

My car is a 2004 Buick LeSabre I bought for $1,500 from Duane, who runs the funeral home. So I drive a funeral director's old car. My 13-year-old brother calls me the Grim Reaper's DJ. Before Duane meets a family, we guess which hymn they'll pick. I'm up 19 to 14. "How Great Thou Art" is the safe bet. I can play it without looking, and I kind of hate it.

Please don't ask me to sing. I can't.

I want to study statistics, and I like a spreadsheet more than most people do. But I've noticed that sometimes I make one so I don't have to feel 48 separate things. If you catch me turning a hard week into a trend line, you're allowed to say so.

My old piano teacher, Arlene, is 86. She listens to the Sunday livestream from assisted living with the volume all the way up, then texts me in all caps: "BREATHE WITH THEM." I still don't know what that means. I think I'm close. Maybe you'll figure it out first.

Thea
