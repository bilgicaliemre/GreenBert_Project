The Tail

For five years I was the back half of a lion. Saturdays started at 7:30 in Chú Tâm's transmission shop on Wilcrest, under two cars on lifts. Chú Tâm played the drum. His daughter Bảo-Trân, my best friend, was the head. I held her belt and followed her feet, since that's all you can see in there. I'm Mexican, so people did a double take when the tail came off.

I got the spot in seventh grade because I could squat with Bảo standing on my legs. I always said I liked the tail because it was chill. Nobody sees your face.

Someone posted a TikTok from a grand opening: the tail comes off, it's me. 31,400 views. I checked it every day for a month. Nine days after it went up, I skipped my first Saturday. By Tết I had missed six of the last eight, and at a bakery in February, with Bảo on my thighs for the high stand, my left knee shook. I stepped back. Her cousin Khôi, fourteen, replaced me and got her up on his first try.

I think I liked the tail because it let me hide. The video was the first time anyone saw me be the tail, and I stopped going.

Chú Tâm wants an answer by Thanksgiving. I can't tell if I'd go back to fix last February or because I miss it. I still sleep in the furry tail pants. He never asked for them back.
