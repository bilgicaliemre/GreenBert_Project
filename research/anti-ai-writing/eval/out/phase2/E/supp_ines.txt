On Tuesdays and Thursdays after school, my grandmother and I do flashcards. Avó Rosa had a stroke two years ago and has aphasia: she understands more than she can say. I made the cards, with pictures, in Portuguese. She can't say my name yet, but she can say "sapato." She's relearning words and I'm learning them for the first time; she's better than me at "agulha" (needle), I'm better at "telemóvel."

I read a lot about aphasia now. I have a stutter, and I kind of wanted stuttering and aphasia to be connected, but they're totally different things.

At my dad's shoe repair shop I read heels, and my heel notebook has 61 entries. One woman's right heel was ground down far more than her left, and it turned out she drives for work.

Heels and Avó are both about figuring out what's going on with someone from the outside, when they can't tell you. With heels it's easy. With Avó it isn't: when she gets a word wrong, I can't tell what she's thinking. I don't know if that's a real connection or if I just want it to be one, like the stutter and the aphasia.

I want to study speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics, or something with how the brain gets words back. I asked Ms. Halvorsen, my middle school speech therapist, if I'd be "allowed" to be one with a stutter. She said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.
