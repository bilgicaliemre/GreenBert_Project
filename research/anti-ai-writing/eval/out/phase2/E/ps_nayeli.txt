For five years I held my best friend's belt and watched her feet. On our lion dance team, Bảo-Trân is the head; her dad, Chú Tâm, plays the drum; and I was the tail. I got the job in seventh grade because I was the only one who could squat while Bảo, all four-foot-eleven of her, stood on my legs. I'm Mexican, so people do a double take when the tail comes off.

Last February, over Tết, we had fourteen shows in three days. At the second show, a new bakery, the lettuce and red envelope were taped high on the awning. That meant the high stand: Bảo steps onto my thighs, and I lift her by the belt onto my shoulders. My left knee started shaking. I stepped back, and she had to come down. The lion head hit the awning, and the mirror on its forehead popped off and rolled under a Camry. On the second try I couldn't get her past my thighs. The owner's son finally held the lettuce out on a broom handle, and the lion ate it off the broom in front of about sixty people.

When the mirror rolled away, I laughed, a nervous, stupid laugh, and inside the lion you hear everything. It sounds like I didn't care. I did, and maybe that's why I laughed. Bảo thought I was laughing at her. She never said so, but in the van she didn't look at me, and she always looks at me in the van.

Chú Tâm put Bảo's cousin Khôi in as tail. He's fourteen, maybe 110 pounds. In the van I announced, "He's gonna fold like a lawn chair." That night he got Bảo up on the first try, and everybody screamed. I was on cymbals, which is what they give the little kids. Khôi did the other twelve shows. I never apologized; I Venmo'd him ten dollars with the note "for takis."

Nobody knew why my knee shook. I'd skipped six of the last eight Saturday practices before Tết and told Chú Tâm it was work, which it was, because I'd asked Itzel to trade shifts so it would be. So my legs weren't ready; that part isn't deep. The other part is a TikTok from a December grand opening: the tail comes off and it's me, under the caption "the back half of the lion is in my AP Lang class" and a skull emoji. It was funny, whatever. I also checked it every day for about a month (31,400 views). I'm not saying that's why, but the first Saturday I skipped was nine days after it went up.

When my knee started shaking, I was wondering whether anyone was filming. At Tết, everybody is. A fall would end up on somebody's phone, but a step back looks like a decision, so I picked what would look better on video. Bảo could have landed on the curb; she didn't, but I didn't know that then.

I've always said I like being the tail because nobody sees your face, and I say it about the Friday PA announcements too. I think what I liked was hiding, which isn't the same. The TikTok was the first time anybody actually saw me be the tail, and then I stopped going. And the lawn chair: I needed Khôi to fold, because if he folded, the position was just hard, and if he didn't, it was me.

Tết is February 6th, and Chú Tâm wants an answer by Thanksgiving. Khôi is the tail now, so I'd be second tail or back on cymbals. I can't tell if I'd go back to fix last February or because I actually miss it. Bảo and I text every day, about everything except the laugh. I still have the furry tail pants. Chú Tâm never asked for them back. I sleep in them.
