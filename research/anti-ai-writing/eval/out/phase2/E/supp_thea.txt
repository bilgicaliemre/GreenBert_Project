Dear Roommate,

I'm the organist at Immanuel Lutheran in Hartington, Nebraska, and the funeral home calls me too. I play the pedals on our 1958 Wicks in socks because my shoes slip; a kid at a funeral lunch said, "That lady has cats on her feet," which, yes.

If my phone lights up with all-caps texts, that's Arlene. She's 86, played organ at Immanuel for 51 years, and taught me pedals. She listens to the livestream from assisted living and texts things like "TOO FAST ON DOXOLOGY. BREATHE WITH THEM." I still don't know exactly what breathe with them means, though I think I'm close.

I want to study statistics or demography, a word I learned after posting a chart on Facebook: Hartington empty by about 2070, "Just math!" Kathy Wieseler, who runs a daycare, commented, "Come count my 22 kids, honey." I replied, "Anecdotes aren't data." I'd only counted my church. I deleted it. My mom, the rural mail carrier, made me write Kathy an apology and put a real stamp on it, then delivered it with the mail. I think I wanted the town to be dying, so leaving wouldn't really be leaving anybody, and I can't tell yet whether I'm coming back after college.

My brother calls me "the Grim Reaper's DJ." I'm up 19 to 14 on Duane, the funeral director, guessing which hymn families will pick.

I've played 48 funerals since June 2024 and never once been to one as just a guest.

Thea
