Tree 23 was my best wild apple. I have 37 tagged in the foothills past Talgar, and wild ones run about 9 to 15 Brix. Tree 23 hit 15.2. I took a bag to Zhanna Serikovna, a researcher at the botanical garden. She cut one open, looked for ten seconds, and said, "This one has been to the city." It grows 30 meters from a picnic spot, probably a hybrid with some dacha apple.

I wanted to argue. I had 15.2 in my notebook, as if that proved where a tree came from. On the bus home I ate three, and they were really good.

Since then I keep coming back to what "wild" means. Malus sieversii, the ancestor of basically every apple, just grows here. Apples don't grow true from seed, so every Aport, Almaty's famous apple, is technically one tree cloned for about 150 years. The wild forests are shrinking, though I don't fully trust the "70 percent gone since the 1960s" line every website copies. Some places cut out hybrids so they don't pollinate wild trees.

If Tree 23 were my decision, I don't know if I'd cut it. Probably the correct answer is yes. My grandmother calls me shala-kazakh, half-Kazakh, because I answer her in Russian, and I think that's part of why I got defensive for a tree.

I want to study conservation genetics and be the person who decides which trees get saved. I don't know yet what that job is called.
