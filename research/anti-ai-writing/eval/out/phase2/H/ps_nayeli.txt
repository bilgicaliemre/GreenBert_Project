For five years I was the back half of the lion (the butt, everybody says it), holding Bảo-Trân's belt and following her feet. Bảo is the head, and her dad, Chú Tâm, plays the drum. I got the job in seventh grade because I was the only one who could squat while Bảo, all four-foot-eleven of her, stood on my legs.

During Tết this February we had fourteen shows in three days. At the second, a new bakery, the owner had taped the lettuce and red envelope high on the awning. That meant the high stand: Bảo steps onto my thighs and I lift her by the belt onto my shoulders. My left knee started shaking. I stepped back, and she had to come down. The lion head hit the awning, and the little mirror on its forehead popped off and rolled under a Camry. On the second try I couldn't get her past my thighs. The owner's son held the lettuce out on a broom handle, and the lion ate it off the broom in front of about sixty people.

When the mirror rolled away, I laughed, a nervous, stupid laugh, and inside the lion you hear everything. Bảo didn't say anything, but in the van she didn't look at me, and she always looks at me in the van. It sounds like I didn't care. I did. Maybe that's why I laughed. I don't know.

Chú Tâm put Bảo's cousin Khôi in as tail. He's fourteen, maybe 110 pounds. In the van I told everybody, "He's gonna fold like a lawn chair." That night he got Bảo up on the first try and everybody screamed. I was on cymbals, which is what they give the little kids. Khôi did the other twelve shows. I never apologized. I Venmo'd him ten dollars with the note "for takis," and I guess that was the apology.

Nobody knew why my knee shook. I'd skipped six of the last eight Saturday practices before Tết. I told Chú Tâm it was work, and it was, because I'd asked a coworker to trade shifts so it would be.

In December someone posted a TikTok of a grand opening we did. The tail comes off and it's me, a Mexican girl whose mom is from Zacatecas, under the caption "the back half of the lion is in my AP Lang class" and a skull emoji. I said it was funny, whatever, then checked it daily for a month: 31,400 views. The first Saturday I skipped was nine days after it went up.

When my knee started shaking, I wondered if anyone was filming. At Tết everybody is. A fall would end up on someone's phone, and a step back looks like a decision, so I picked the one that would look better on video. Bảo could have landed on the curb. She didn't, but I didn't know that when I stepped.

I always said I liked being the tail because nobody sees your face. I say that about the Friday PA announcements too. I think now that what I liked was hiding. The TikTok was the first time anyone really saw me be the tail, and I stopped showing up. I needed Khôi to fold, too. If he folded, the position was just hard. If he didn't, it was me.

Tết is February 6th, and Chú Tâm wants an answer by Thanksgiving. Khôi is the tail now, so I'd be second tail or back on cymbals. I can't tell yet whether I'd go back to fix last February or because I miss it. Bảo and I text every day about everything except the laugh. Whatever I tell Chú Tâm, I want it to be a choice I'd make with people watching. I still have the furry tail pants. He never asked for them back, and I sleep in them.
