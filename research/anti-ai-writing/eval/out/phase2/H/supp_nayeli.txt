Until this year, my Saturdays started at 7:30 a.m. in the back bay of Chú Tâm's transmission shop on Wilcrest, two cars on the lifts above our lion dance team. Chú Tâm plays the drum, his daughter Bảo-Trân is the head, and I'm the tail. I hold her belt and see only her feet and the floor, so I follow her feet.

For five years I said I liked the tail because nobody sees your face. In December a TikTok showed the tail coming off at a grand opening, and it was me. It got 31,400 views. Nine days later I skipped my first Saturday. I missed six of the last eight before Tết. At a bakery that February, Bảo was on my thighs for the high stand when my left knee shook. My first thought was whether anyone was filming. I stepped back, because a step back looks like a decision. She could have landed on the curb.

Chú Tâm put in Khôi, who's fourteen. In the van I said he'd fold like a lawn chair. He got her up first try that night while I played cymbals.

I say the same about reading the Friday announcements on the PA: nobody sees you. I think I liked the tail because it let me hide. Chú Tâm wants an answer about next Tết by Thanksgiving. I'd be second tail or cymbals, and I can't tell whether I'd go back to fix last February or because I miss it.
