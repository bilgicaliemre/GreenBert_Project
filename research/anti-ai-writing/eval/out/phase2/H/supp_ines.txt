On Tuesdays and Thursdays after school, my grandmother and I go through picture flashcards I made in Portuguese. Avó Rosa had a stroke two years ago and has aphasia now, so she understands more than she can say. She's relearning the words and I'm learning them for the first time. She's better than me at "agulha," needle; I'm better at "telemóvel." She can't say my name yet. She can say "sapato."

Now I read a lot about aphasia and how the brain gets words back. I learned stuttering and aphasia are totally different things. I have a stutter, and I had kind of wanted our trouble with words to be connected.

At my dad's shoe repair shop, I read heels. A worn outer edge means the customer supinates. One woman's right heel was ground down more than her left, and it turned out she drives for work. My heel notebook has 61 entries. Heels and flashcards both mean figuring out what's going on with someone from the outside, when they can't tell you. Heels are easy. With Avó, when she gets a word wrong, I can't tell what she's thinking. I don't know if that connection is real or one I just want, like the stutter and the aphasia.

I want to study speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics. When I asked my old speech therapist if I'd be "allowed" to do her job with a stutter, she said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.
