Dear Roommate,

I'm the organist at Immanuel Lutheran in Hartington, Nebraska, and the funeral home calls me too. Since June 2024 I've played 48 funerals on a 1958 Wicks, in socks, because my shoes slip on the pedals. At one funeral lunch a little kid pointed at me and said, "That lady has cats on her feet."

The all-caps texts on Sundays are from Arlene. She was the organist at Immanuel for 51 years, and when her eyes got bad she taught me pedals. She's 86 now and texts me from assisted living after the livestream: "TOO FAST ON DOXOLOGY. BREATHE WITH THEM." I still don't know what that means. I think I'm close.

I want to study statistics, or demography, a word I learned after I posted a chart on Facebook predicting Hartington would be empty by 2070. I'd only counted my own church. My mom, the rural mail carrier, made me write an apology, put a real stamp on it, and delivered it with the mail. Later I realized part of me wanted the town to be dying, so leaving wouldn't mean leaving anybody. I still don't know if I'm going back.

Duane, the funeral director, and I guess which hymn each family will pick. I'm up 19 to 14. My brother Lukas calls me the Grim Reaper's DJ, though I can't sing at all.

I've never been to a funeral as just a guest. If you ever need someone at one, I'll sit with you.

Thea
