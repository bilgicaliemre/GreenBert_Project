The Back Half of the Lion

For five years, my Saturdays started at 7:30 a.m. in the back bay of Chú Tâm's transmission shop on Wilcrest, two cars up on the lifts above our lion dance troupe. Chú Tâm plays the drum, his daughter Bảo-Trân is the head, and I'm the tail. I hold her belt. Inside the lion I see only her feet and the floor, so I follow her feet.

I always said I liked being the tail because nobody sees your face. Last winter, a TikTok of me coming out of the tail reached 31,400 views. Nine days after it went up, I skipped my first practice; I missed six of the last eight before Tết. At a bakery opening, during the high stand, my left knee shook with Bảo on my thighs. My first thought was who was filming, so I stepped back, because a step back looks like a decision. She could have landed on the curb.

Fourteen-year-old Khôi took my place. In the van I said he'd fold like a lawn chair. He got her up on his first try that night, while I played cymbals.

Being the tail didn't make me humble. It let me call hiding "chill." Chú Tâm wants my answer about next Tết by Thanksgiving; I'd be second tail now, or cymbals. If I go back, I want it to be because I miss the lion, not to fix last February.
