Dear Roommate,

Some things before we share a room.

I'm the organist at Immanuel Lutheran in Hartington, Nebraska, and the funeral home calls me too. I've played 48 funerals since June 2024 on a 1958 Wicks. I play the pedals in socks because my shoes slip. A kid at a funeral lunch once pointed and said, "That lady has cats on her feet." Accurate.

If my phone lights up on a Sunday with all-caps texts, that's Arlene. She's 86, played organ at Immanuel for 51 years, taught me pedals, and now listens to the livestream from assisted living. Her latest: "TOO FAST ON DOXOLOGY. BREATHE WITH THEM." I think I'm close.

I want to study statistics or demography. I learned that second word after posting a chart on Facebook predicting my town would be empty by 2070. I'd only counted my own church. I deleted it, and my mom, the rural mail carrier, made me write an apology, put a real stamp on it, and delivered it with the mail. Later I realized part of me wanted the town to be dying, so leaving wouldn't mean leaving anybody. I still don't know if I'm going back.

Also: I can't sing at all. My brother calls me "the Grim Reaper's DJ." I'm up 19 to 14 on our funeral director at guessing which hymn families pick.

I've never been to a funeral as just a guest. If you ever need someone at one, I'll sit with you.

Thea
