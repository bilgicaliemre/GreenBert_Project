The Back Half of the Lion

For five years I held Bảo-Trân's belt and watched her feet. The tail sees only the floor and the dancer ahead. Bảo is the head; her dad, Chú Tâm, plays the drum. I got the job in seventh grade because I was the only one who could squat while Bảo, all four-foot-eleven of her, stood on my legs.

This February, during Tết, we had fourteen shows in three days. At the second, a new bakery, the lettuce and red envelope were taped high on the awning, which meant the high stand: Bảo steps onto my thighs, and I lift her by the belt onto my shoulders. My left knee started shaking. I stepped back, and she had to come down. The lion head hit the awning, and the mirror on its forehead popped off and rolled under a Camry. On the second try I couldn't get her past my thighs. The owner's son finally held the lettuce out on a broom handle, and the lion ate it off the broom in front of about sixty people.

When the mirror rolled away, I laughed, a nervous, stupid laugh, and inside the lion you hear everything. Bảo didn't say anything, but in the van she didn't look at me, and she always looks at me in the van.

Chú Tâm put Bảo's cousin Khôi in as tail. He's fourteen, maybe 110 pounds. In the van I announced, "He's gonna fold like a lawn chair." That night he got Bảo up on the first try, and everybody screamed. I was on cymbals, which is what they give the little kids. Khôi did the other twelve shows. I never apologized. I Venmo'd him ten dollars with the note "for takis."

Bad luck is the easy explanation. Here are the dates. I skipped six of the last eight Saturday practices before Tết. I told Chú Tâm it was work, which was true because I'd asked a coworker to trade shifts so it would be.

Someone had posted a TikTok from a grand opening we did in December. The tail comes off and it's me, a Mexican girl whose mom is from Zacatecas, under the caption "the back half of the lion is in my AP Lang class" and a skull emoji. I told everyone it was funny. I checked it every day for a month: 31,400 views. The first Saturday I skipped was nine days after it went up.

When my knee started shaking, I was wondering whether anyone was filming. At Tết, everybody is. A fall would end up on someone's phone, but a step back looks like a decision, so I picked what would look better on video. Bảo could have landed on the curb. She didn't, but I didn't know that when I stepped.

I always said I liked being the tail because nobody sees your face. I say the same about doing the Friday PA announcements. I think now that what I liked was hiding. That isn't the same thing. The TikTok was the first time anyone really saw me as the tail, and I stopped showing up. Even the lawn chair line makes sense now. I needed Khôi to fold. If he folded, the position was just hard. If he didn't, it was me.

Tết is February 6th, and Chú Tâm wants an answer by Thanksgiving. Khôi is the tail now, so I'd be second tail or back on cymbals. I can't yet tell whether I'd return to fix last February or because I miss it. Bảo and I text every day about everything except the laugh.

I've learned that a step back can look like a decision without being one. Whatever I tell Chú Tâm, I want it to be a real one, made where people can see me. I still have the furry tail pants. He never asked for them back.
