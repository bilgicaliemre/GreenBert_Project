On Tuesdays and Thursdays, I do the flashcards I made for my avó, Rosa. A stroke two years ago left her with aphasia, and she understands more than she can say. She's relearning words I'm learning for the first time: she beats me at "agulha" (needle); I beat her at "telemóvel" (cell phone). I want to know how a brain gets words back.

I stutter, especially on hard sounds like the C in Carvalho, the name of my dad's shoe repair shop. I read a lot about aphasia now, and I kind of wanted it connected to my stutter. They're totally different things.

I can tell how someone walks from heel wear; my heel notebook has 61 entries, starting with my dad's work boots. One woman's right heel was ground down far more than her left. She drives for work, and that heel rests by the pedal. Heels and avó are the same problem to me: figuring out what's going on with someone from the outside when they can't tell you. With heels it's easy. With avó it isn't. She can say "sapato" (shoe) but not my name yet, and sometimes she calls me Lurdes, my aunt who died before I was born. I used to correct her. Now I don't, and I still can't tell what she's thinking when she gets a word wrong.

I don't know if heels and avó are really connected or if I just want them to be, like my stutter and aphasia.
