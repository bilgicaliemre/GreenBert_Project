What my keyword rules can't see in an ESG report

"We are committed to a sustainable future."

"We reduced Scope 1 and 2 emissions by 4% against our 2019 baseline."

Both sentences sound responsible. Only the second can be checked, because it gives an amount and a year to compare against. My program labels the first one Vague and the second one Strong. Telling those two apart was the easy part.

I'm a high-school student and the first author of GreenBERT, a research project with a university co-author. It looks for possible greenwashing in corporate ESG (environmental, social, governance) reports. The stage I've built so far uses rules. It turns a PDF into text with page numbers (PyMuPDF) and splits the text into sentences (NLTK). A sentence gets through the "gate" only if it has an ESG topic word plus either an action verb, including forms like "we reduced", or a hard quantity such as a number with a unit. A topic word on its own counts as description, and the sentence is dropped.

The gate has two conditions. After it come ten filters, one for each kind of sentence that gets past the gate and still isn't a claim: boilerplate, reference-dense text, navigation and headers, broken fragments, policy content, governance role descriptions, activity reports, methodology text, general statements and risk descriptions. For broken fragments, a part-of-speech tagger checks whether there is a verb at all. Every rejected sentence is saved with its drop reason, so I can audit what I threw away. On one Unilever report the program keeps 583 claims; on a Dr Pepper report it keeps 218.

The best precision number I have is 92%, on a development set of 26 sentences. I tuned the filters on those same 26, so it isn't a fair test. An independent manual review of 100 claims from an earlier version (v3) found 67% were real claims. AI agents that audited the full output were stricter than people: 33% for v3, 39% for v4.0 and 44% for v4 final. An audit of 1,767 rejected sentences put recall at 48% for v4.0, which means that version dropped more than half of the real claims. In v4 final it reached 73%. All of these numbers are in-sample, so they're optimistic.

Under the strict audit, fewer than half of the sentences v4 final keeps are claims, and the errors that remain are about meaning. A rule can see a past-tense verb and a percentage. It can't see whether the sentence reports something the company did or explains how a number was calculated, and that is the difference a reader needs.

So the next stage is a BERT classifier, which I haven't built yet. The end goal is a public website where anyone can upload an ESG report PDF and get back a greenwashing assessment with an explanation. The two sentences at the top of this post are already handled. The classifier is for the sentences my rules still get wrong.
