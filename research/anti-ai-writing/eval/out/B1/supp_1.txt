My avó, Rosa, can say "sapato." She can't say my name yet.

She had a stroke two years ago and has aphasia. She understands more than she can say. On Tuesdays and Thursdays after school we go through flashcards I made for her, pictures with the words in Portuguese. It's funny, because she's relearning the words and I'm learning them for the first time. She's better than me at "agulha" (needle); I'm better at "telemóvel" (cell phone). Sometimes she calls me Lurdes, which was my aunt's name. Lurdes died before I was born. At first I corrected her, but now I don't.

How does a brain get words back? Why "sapato" and not "Inês"?

I read a lot about aphasia now. I kind of wanted stuttering and aphasia to be connected, and they're not; they're totally different things. My stutter gets worse on the phone, especially on hard sounds like "C," which is a problem when my dad's shoe repair shop is called Carvalho Shoe Service. I used to answer "Hi, shoe repair." Last month I said the whole name on the third try, and the guy on the line waited. He wanted to know if we fix purses (we don't).

I want to study speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics. I asked Ms. Halvorsen, my speech therapist from middle school, whether I'd be "allowed" to be a speech therapist with a stutter. She said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.
