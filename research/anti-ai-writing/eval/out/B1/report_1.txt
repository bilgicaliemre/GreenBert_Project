Claim extraction

Claim extraction, the stage of GreenBERT built so far, is rule-based. Each ESG report is converted from PDF to text with PyMuPDF, with page numbers retained, and the text is split into sentences with NLTK. A sentence passes the gate only if it contains an ESG topic word and, in addition, either an action verb or a hard quantity. Action verbs include first-person and past forms such as "we reduced"; hard quantities are numbers with units and counts. If a sentence has a topic word but neither of the other two features, we treat it as description and drop it.

Sentences that pass the gate then go through non-claim filters. These remove boilerplate, reference-dense text, navigation and headers, broken fragments, policy content, governance role descriptions, activity reports, methodology text, general statements and risk descriptions. For broken fragments, a part-of-speech tagger checks whether the sentence contains a verb. We save every rejected sentence together with its drop reason so that recall can be audited.

Each remaining claim receives four labels. The ESG type (E, S or G) is assigned by keyword dominance. Claim_Type is Future Promise, Strong or Vague, with Strong reserved for claims that state a hard quantity: "We reduced Scope 1 and 2 emissions by 4% against our 2019 baseline" is Strong, whereas "We are committed to a sustainable future" is Vague. Evidence is coded Yes, Partial or No, and each claim also carries a Risk_Signal. Of the six reports loaded, one from Unilever yielded 583 claims and one from Dr Pepper 218.

We measured precision in three ways. On a 26-sentence development set the extractor reached 92%, but the filters were tuned on these sentences, so we do not report this as a headline figure. An independent manual review of 100 claims found 67% precision; it covered an earlier version (v3) and does not measure the final one. Automated audits of the full corpus by AI agents gave 33% for v3, 39% for v4.0 and 44% for v4 final. The agents judge more strictly than human reviewers, which is consistent with the gap between their 33% and the human 67% for v3. Recall, estimated by auditing 1,767 rejected sentences, rose from 48% (v4.0) to 73% (v4 final). All of these figures are in-sample and therefore optimistic. The remaining errors concern what sentences mean, which keyword rules cannot capture; this ceiling is the reason for the next stage, a BERT classifier that has not yet been built.
