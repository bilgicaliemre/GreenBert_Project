Hi, shoe repair (sample essay for a fictional student)

I hate the phone. I have a stutter, and it gets worse on the phone, especially on hard sounds like C. That's a problem, because the shop where I work on Saturdays is called Carvalho Shoe Service, and Carvalho is also my last name. So every time I picked up the shop phone, I said "Hi, shoe repair" instead of the name. My dad noticed after about a year and never said anything about it.

The shop is his. It has been on Pleasant Street in Fall River, Massachusetts, since 1994, which makes it older than I am. I mostly do the counter and write tickets, and since last summer I've been allowed to do heels and half-soles on the finishing machine. Everything in there smells like Barge cement and leather dust, my hoodie included, so my friends say I smell like a cowboy's basement. My dad's right thumb is stained black from the dye. I asked him once if he wanted me to take over the shop, and he said, "The shop isn't a person, it doesn't need anyone." I don't know if that was a yes or a no.

I can also tell how someone walks from the wear on their heels. If the outer edge is worn down, the person supinates. One woman's right heel was ground down far more than her left. When you drive, the right heel rests on the floor by the pedal, so I guessed out loud that she drives for work. She does, and she looked at me like I was a psychic. I keep a notebook of heels (61 entries so far; entry 1 is my dad's work boots).

On Tuesdays and Thursdays after school I do flashcards with my avó, my grandmother, Rosa. She had a stroke two years ago and has aphasia, which means she understands more than she can say. I made the cards for her myself, with pictures, all in Portuguese. It's a little funny, because she is relearning the words and I'm learning them for the first time. My Portuguese is bad at chemistry words, and it turns out it's bad at sewing words too: she's better than me at agulha, needle. I'm better at telemóvel, cell phone.

Sometimes she calls me Lurdes. That was my aunt's name; my aunt died before I was born. At first I corrected her. Now I don't. She can't say my name yet, but she can say sapato, shoe.

I read a lot about aphasia now. One thing I found out is that stuttering and aphasia are totally different things. I had kind of wanted them to be connected, and they're not. I want to study speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics, or something with how the brain gets words back. I asked Ms. Halvorsen, my speech therapist from middle school, whether I would be "allowed" to be a speech therapist with a stutter. She said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.

I can resole a sneaker in 25 minutes. I have never once answered the shop phone with the name. Actually, that's not true anymore. Last month I said "Carvalho Shoe Service" on the third try, and the guy on the other end waited through the first two. He was calling to ask if we fix purses. We don't.
