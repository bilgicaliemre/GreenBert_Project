Carvalho Shoe Service (sample essay for a fictional student)

"Hi, shoe repair." For a long time, that was how I answered the phone at my dad's shop. The shop is on Pleasant Street, it has been there since 1994, and it's called Carvalho Shoe Service. Carvalho is also my last name, which is a problem, because I have a stutter that gets worse on the phone, especially on hard sounds like C. I hate the phone. My dad noticed the "shoe repair" thing after about a year and never said anything about it.

I work there on Saturdays. Mostly I work the counter and do tickets, and since last summer I've been allowed to do heels and half-soles on the finishing machine. I can resole a sneaker in 25 minutes. The shop smells like Barge cement and leather dust, and so does my hoodie (my friends say I smell like a "cowboy's basement"). My dad's right thumb is stained black from the dye. I asked him once if he wanted me to take over the shop, and he said, "The shop isn't a person, it doesn't need anyone." I don't know if that was a yes or a no.

The thing I'm weirdly good at is heels. I can tell how someone walks from the wear on their heel: if the outer edge is worn down, they supinate, which means their weight rolls to the outside of the foot. One woman's right heel was ground down way more than her left, and it turned out she drives for work, so her right heel rests on the floor by the pedal. When I said that out loud, she looked at me like I was a psychic. I keep a notebook of heels. It has 61 entries, and entry 1 is my dad's work boots.

On Tuesdays and Thursdays after school I do flashcards with my grandmother, my avó, Rosa. She had a stroke two years ago and has aphasia: she understands more than she can say. I made the cards for her, with pictures and the words in Portuguese. The funny part is that she's relearning the words and I'm learning them for the first time. She's better than me at agulha (needle); I'm better at telemóvel (cell phone). Sometimes she calls me Lurdes, which was the name of my aunt who died before I was born. At first I corrected her. Now I don't. She can say sapato (shoe), but she can't say my name yet.

I want to study speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics, or something with how the brain gets words back, which is what my grandmother is doing with those flashcards. I read a lot about aphasia now. I found out that stuttering and aphasia are totally different things. I kind of wanted them to be connected, and they're not. I also asked Ms. Halvorsen, my speech therapist from middle school, whether I'd be "allowed" to be a speech therapist with a stutter. She said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.

I have never once answered the shop phone with the name. Actually, that stopped being true last month. I said "Carvalho Shoe Service" on the third try, and the guy on the other end waited through the first two. He was calling to ask if we fix purses. We don't.
