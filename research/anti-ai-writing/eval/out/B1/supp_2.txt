My avó, my grandmother Rosa, is better than me at "agulha" (needle). I'm better at "telemóvel" (cell phone). Every Tuesday and Thursday after school we go through the picture flashcards I made for her in Portuguese.

Two years ago she had a stroke, and now she has aphasia. She understands more than she can say. The funny part is that she's relearning the words on those cards and I'm learning them for the first time.

What I want to understand is how a brain gets words back. I read a lot about aphasia now, and I kind of wanted it to be connected to my stutter, which gets worse on the phone, especially on hard sounds like C. That's a problem at my dad's shop, Carvalho Shoe Service, where I used to answer the phone "Hi, shoe repair" instead of saying the name. Then I found out stuttering and aphasia are totally different things.

I want to study speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics. I asked Ms. Halvorsen, my speech therapist from middle school, if I'd be "allowed" to be a speech therapist with a stutter, and she said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.

Sometimes my grandmother calls me Lurdes. That was my aunt's name; she died before I was born. At first I corrected her, and now I don't. She can't say my name yet. She can say "sapato," shoe.
