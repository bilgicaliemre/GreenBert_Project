On Tuesdays and Thursdays after school, my grandmother Rosa and I go through picture flashcards I made for her in Portuguese. Two years ago she had a stroke, and now she has aphasia: she understands more than she can say. It's funny, because she's relearning words I'm learning for the first time. She's better than me at "agulha" (needle), and I'm better at "telemóvel" (cell phone).

How does a brain get words back? I read a lot about aphasia now. I also have a stutter that gets worse on the phone, especially on hard sounds like C, which is a problem because my dad's shoe repair shop is Carvalho Shoe Service and Carvalho is my last name. On the shop phone I used to say "Hi, shoe repair" instead. I kind of wanted my stutter and Rosa's aphasia to be connected. They're not; they're totally different things. Sometimes she calls me Lurdes, my aunt who died before I was born, and at first I corrected her; now I don't. What the stutter and the aphasia do have in common is my name. Last month I got "Carvalho Shoe Service" out on the third try; Rosa can say "sapato" (shoe), but not "Inês" yet.

I want to study speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics. I asked Ms. Halvorsen, my speech therapist from middle school, whether I'd be "allowed" to be a speech therapist with a stutter. She said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.
