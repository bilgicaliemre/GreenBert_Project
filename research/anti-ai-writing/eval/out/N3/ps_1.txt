I used to answer the phone at my dad's shop with "Hi, shoe repair." The shop is Carvalho Shoe Service, on Pleasant Street since 1994. I work there on Saturdays, mostly the counter and the tickets, and since last summer I'm allowed to do heels and half-soles on the finishing machine. It smells like Barge cement and leather dust, and so does my hoodie; my friends say I smell like a "cowboy's basement." I can tell how someone walks from the wear on their heels. One woman's right heel was ground down way more than her left, and it turned out she drives for work, so that heel rests on the floor by the pedal. I said it out loud and she looked at me like I was a psychic. I keep a notebook of heels; it has 61 entries, and entry 1 is my dad's work boots.

I hate the phone. I have a stutter that gets worse on it, especially on hard sounds like the C in Carvalho. My dad noticed after about a year and never said anything about it.

Sophomore year I got a 54 on my first AP Chem test and told my mom it was an 85. I kept it up for three weeks. I even forged the parent signature on the progress report, which was really bad. Then my teacher, Mr. Oduya, emailed home. My mom didn't yell. She asked me to explain the test to her, question by question, at the kitchen table, in Portuguese, and I'm not good at chemistry words in Portuguese. It took two hours. I didn't know the word for "mole," so I said "the unit thing, like a dozen," and she laughed at that part.

I ended the class with a B+. After that I made a rule: I keep a list on my phone of every lie I tell, even small ones like "I'm almost there." I'm still not sure if the list makes me more honest or just better at noticing. It has 212 items since October of sophomore year, and most of them are about being on time.

On Tuesdays and Thursdays after school I do flashcards with my avó, Rosa. She had a stroke two years ago and has aphasia, and she understands more than she can say. I made the cards with pictures, in Portuguese. It's funny, because she's relearning the words and I'm learning them for the first time; she's better than me at "agulha" (needle), and I'm better at "telemóvel" (cell phone). Sometimes she calls me Lurdes, the name of my aunt, who died before I was born. At first I corrected her, and now I don't. My dad never said anything about "Hi, shoe repair" either. She can't say my name yet, but she can say "sapato" (shoe).

I read a lot about aphasia now. I found out that stuttering and aphasia are totally different things. I kind of wanted them to be connected, and they're not. I asked Ms. Halvorsen, my speech therapist from middle school, if I'd be "allowed" to be a speech therapist with a stutter, and she said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet, but speech-language pathology is what I want to study, maybe linguistics, or something about how the brain gets words back.

I asked my dad once if he wanted me to take over the shop. He said, "The shop isn't a person, it doesn't need anyone." I don't know if that was a yes or a no. Last month I answered the shop phone with "Carvalho Shoe Service." It took three tries, and the guy on the other end waited. He wanted to know if we fix purses, which we don't.
