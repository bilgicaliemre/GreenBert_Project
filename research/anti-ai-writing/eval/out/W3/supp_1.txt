My avó Rosa can say "sapato," but she can't say my name yet. A stroke two years ago left her with aphasia: she understands more than she can say. Tuesdays and Thursdays we do Portuguese picture flashcards I made for her. She's relearning the words while I learn them for the first time; she's better at "agulha" (needle), and I'm better at "telemóvel" (cell phone).

I read a lot now about aphasia and how a brain gets words back. I have a stutter, and I kind of wanted stuttering and aphasia to be connected, but they're totally different things.

At my dad's shoe repair shop, I can tell how someone walks from the wear on their heel. One woman's right heel was ground down way more than her left; she drives for work, and that heel rests on the floor by the pedal. My notebook of heels has 61 entries. Heels and my avó both come down to figuring out what's going on with someone from the outside, when they can't tell you. With heels it's easy. With her it isn't: when she gets a word wrong, I can't tell what she's thinking. I don't know if that's a real connection or one I just want, like the stutter and the aphasia.

I asked Ms. Halvorsen, my middle school speech therapist, if I'd be "allowed" to be one with a stutter. She said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet.
