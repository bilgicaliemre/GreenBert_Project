My avó, Rosa, calls me Lurdes sometimes. Lurdes was my aunt, who died before I was born. At first I corrected her; now I don't. A stroke two years ago left her with aphasia: she understands more than she can say. Tuesdays and Thursdays after school, we do Portuguese picture flashcards I made for her. She's relearning words while I learn them for the first time, so she's better at agulha (needle) and I'm better at telemóvel (cell phone). She can say sapato (shoe). She can't say my name yet.

I read a lot about aphasia now, because I want to know how the brain gets words back. I have a stutter, and I kind of wanted stuttering and aphasia to be connected, but they're totally different things.

At my dad's shoe repair shop, I can tell how someone walks from the wear on their heels. My notebook of heels has 61 entries. One woman's right heel was ground down way more than her left, and it turned out she drives for work, so that heel rests on the floor by the pedal.

Heels and my avó are both about figuring out what's going on with someone from the outside, when they can't tell you. With heels it's easy. With her it isn't: when she gets a word wrong, I can't tell what she's thinking. I don't know if that's a real connection or if I just want it to be one, like the stutter and the aphasia.
