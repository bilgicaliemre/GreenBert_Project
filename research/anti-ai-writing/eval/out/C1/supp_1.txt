On Tuesdays and Thursdays after school, my avó Rosa and I go through Portuguese picture flashcards I made for her. Two years ago she had a stroke, and now she has aphasia: she understands more than she can say. She's relearning words I'm learning for the first time. She's better than me at "agulha" (needle); I'm better at "telemóvel" (cell phone).

I want to know how the brain gets words back, so I read a lot about aphasia now. I kind of wanted it to be connected to my stutter, which gets worse on hard sounds like "C." It isn't. Stuttering and aphasia are totally different things.

Some days Avó calls me Lurdes, the aunt who died before I was born. I used to correct her; now I don't. She can't say my name yet, but she can say "sapato," shoe. Why does one word come back and another not? I'd like to study that in speech-language pathology, maybe linguistics.

I asked Ms. Halvorsen, my speech therapist from middle school, if I'd be "allowed" to be a speech therapist with a stutter. She said some of the best ones stutter. I don't fully believe her yet. On Saturdays I work the counter at my dad's shoe repair shop, and I used to answer the phone "Hi, shoe repair" so I wouldn't have to say "Carvalho." Last month I said "Carvalho Shoe Service" on the third try, and the man waited. He wanted to know if we fix purses. We don't.
