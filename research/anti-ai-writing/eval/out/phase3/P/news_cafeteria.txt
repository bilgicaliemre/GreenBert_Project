Foam Trays Out, Reusable Trays In, but Third Lunch Is Still Waiting for Dry Ones

Foam lunch trays are gone from the Quillmere Valley High School cafeteria. Since Monday, Oct. 5, students have been eating off hard plastic trays that go back to the kitchen dishwasher instead of into the trash.

The only notice of the change was a flyer posted by the cafeteria door on Friday, Oct. 2. Several students the Ledger spoke with said they never saw it.

Cafeteria manager Renata Valdoro-Pike, who has worked at QVHS for 11 years, said the amount of waste pushed her to make the switch.

"We were filling a dumpster with foam every week. I couldn't look at it anymore," she said in an interview on Oct. 14.

The cafeteria serves about 1,150 lunches a day across three lunch periods. Foam trays cost about 6 cents each, which Valdoro-Pike estimates added up to roughly $12,400 a year over 180 school days.

The cafeteria bought 1,400 reusable trays from Brindlemark Foodservice Supply for $4,620, or $3.30 per tray. A return cart and an extra dish rack cost another $1,180. A district "facilities sustainability" grant paid for the purchase, and lunch still costs $3.25.

Washing the trays takes an extra part-time dishwasher shift of two hours a day. Valdoro-Pike would not say what the shift pays, saying only that it is "in the budget."

By her count, the cafeteria's trash dropped from about 22 bags a day to about nine during the program's first eight school days, Oct. 5 through Oct. 14.

Students are split on the new trays. Junior Theo Marchetti-Ross, who writes sports for the Ledger, likes them.

"They don't bend when you put the chili on them. That alone is worth it," he said.

Senior Priya Sandoval-Hune does not. "Third lunch gets the trays that are still wet. Nobody wants a wet tray," she said.

The dishwasher can't keep up after second lunch, so third lunch, which starts at 12:40, gets trays that are still damp. Valdoro-Pike said she is "looking at" buying 200 more trays, but there is no date or cost yet, and the purchase would need district approval.

During third lunch on Thursday, Oct. 15, about eight people were in line at the return cart around 1:05 p.m. Two students stacked trays with food still on them. One tray was dropped, cracked at the corner and went back into the rack anyway. About one in four trays a Ledger reporter picked up felt damp.

As of Oct. 14, 37 trays were missing.

"If you have one in your locker, just bring it back. No questions," Valdoro-Pike said.
