Oleh Pro2 Glovo

There is a contact in my phone saved as "Oleh Pro2 Glovo." Since March I have opened BLIK, the payment app, about four times to send him 230 złoty. The money isn't the problem. The problem is the message box, because I would have to write something in it, and I still don't know what.

I fix broken e-scooters in the bike room under our building, the falowiec in Gdańsk's Przymorze, a block about 850 meters long that tourists photograph. My corner has two shelves, a folding table from Lidl and one socket by the door. I buy dead Xiaomis and Ninebots on OLX, repair them and sell them as KacperFix. Forty-one scooters since summer 2024.

In March, Oleh, a Ukrainian courier for Glovo, brought me his Pro 2 because the battery was dying on hills. Handing it over, he said, "I need it for work, it must be good." One group of ten cells read 2.9 volts; the rest were almost 4. I replaced them with salvaged cells from a scooter I'd bought in Chełm. Two tested weak on my charger. I thought: it's fine, it's a mix. Spot welders cost 400 złoty, so I soldered the strips. He paid 380, and I gave him a handwritten card: "gwarancja 3 miesiące," three months' warranty.

On day nineteen, at 22:10, the scooter cut out on Grunwaldzka near Galeria Bałtycka, in the rain, with an order still in his bag. He pushed it about four kilometers to my building and came down to the bike room almost at midnight, wet.

When he walked in, I was thinking about the 380. Before I unscrewed anything I said, "It got wet." Then I opened it in front of him. There was a little water at the bottom, as there always is in Gdańsk, and I pointed at it. I gave him 150 back and said that was fair because of the water. He said "dobra," okay, and walked out talking on the phone in Ukrainian. I don't know what he said.

Two days later I opened the pack properly. One of my solder joints had cracked, and I had cooked the cell next to it. It was the repair, not the rain. Last winter, on a Facebook group about scooters, I had argued for three days that you can solder cells if you're fast enough. In April I deleted those comments.

The technical problem was easy. A month later I bought a spot welder for 280 złoty and a fire extinguisher that now hangs by the socket. I also stopped doing batteries for other people. That sounds like a lesson, but really I just stopped doing the part where I can mess up.

The part I'd rather leave out: I used salvaged cells because new ones would have eaten my margin, and I knew he needed the scooter for work. I knew both at the same time. Once I divided my profit by my hours and got about seven złoty an hour. Żabka pays more. That is the margin I was protecting.

And batteries are exactly what I want to study. The controller and the battery board are the interesting part of a scooter, the reason I want electrical engineering, and also the part I'm worst at. Quitting can't be the whole answer, but I don't know yet what is. My best guess: use the right tools for the dangerous part, and when I'm wrong, say so before I start calculating.

I saw Oleh in June on Grunwaldzka, riding a new Ninebot that wasn't from me. I didn't wave. He never left a review on OLX, and I still check sometimes. So this is my draft for the message box:

It wasn't the rain. It was my solder joint, and I knew you needed it for work. Here is the rest of your money.
