Our graph of T² against L came out as a straight line with an intercept of about -0.002, close enough to zero to say the data fit T = 2π√(L/g). The slope was 4.105 s²/m, giving g = 9.62 m/s². That is about 1.9% below the textbook value of 9.81 m/s². The shape was right, but all five mean periods were a little longer than the formula predicts. At 1.00 m we expected 2.006 s and measured 2.026 s.

Random error would scatter points both ways, so the cause is probably systematic, and the string is my main suspect. The proper string had run out, so we used cotton twine from the art room, and it stretched. The 1.00 m length read about 1.01 m after the bob had hung for a few minutes. Using 1.01 m with g = 9.81 m/s², the predicted period becomes about 2.016 s, which covers roughly half the gap. We never re-measured the other lengths, so they may have stretched too. Release angle may have added a little. Below 0.60 m we judged 10 degrees by eye, and a wider swing has a slightly longer period than the simple formula allows.

Reaction time can't explain the long periods. My ruler-drop result was about 0.2 s, and the spread at each length was up to 0.15 s over ten swings, or around 0.015 s per swing. Jonah thinks I started the stopwatch late a couple of times. A late start would make the time shorter, though, and we didn't record which trials it was.

We left out trial 2 at 0.60 m (14.11 s) because Jonah lost count when Mr. Pell came in. Dividing 14.11 by 9 gives 1.568 s, nearly matching the 1.569 s from the other two trials, so it was almost certainly nine swings. Including it drops R² from 1.000 to 0.997 but barely moves the slope. The 0.20 m trial 3 was redone at the end of the lesson, with the string loaded for about 40 minutes, and it landed between the other two trials with no obvious sign of extra stretch.

Next time I would use a less stretchy string and measure each length with the bob already hanging. Jonah's idea of a phone light-gate app would take my reaction time out of the timing, and a protractor at every length would keep the release angle consistent.
