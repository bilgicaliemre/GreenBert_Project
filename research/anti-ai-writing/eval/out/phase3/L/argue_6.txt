Let Homework Be Practice

Every school night, somewhere around ten o'clock, a kitchen table turns into a negotiation. A student has three worksheets left, a group chat full of answers, and a choice to make: struggle through the problems alone and maybe get some wrong, or copy what a friend posted and get full credit. When homework counts toward a grade, that choice is not close. I think homework should be practice only, because grading it punishes the mistakes it exists to catch, rewards copying over learning, and ends up measuring a student's home life more than what they understand.

Start with what homework is for. A teacher assigns twenty math problems so students can try a new skill before the test, while being wrong is still cheap. You learn to factor a polynomial by doing it badly a few times and figuring out where you slipped. Once those twenty problems are worth points, though, being wrong gets expensive. A student who honestly attempts the work and misunderstands one step loses points for the very thing practice is supposed to allow. Imagine a basketball coach who counted every missed free throw at practice against your season average. Nobody would shoot.

Grading homework also teaches students to fake it. Everyone in my grade knows how this works. Someone finishes the chemistry worksheet, snaps a photo, and by first period half the class has the same answers in slightly different handwriting. The grade book says they all understood stoichiometry. The test a week later says otherwise. If points go to finished work that nobody watched you finish, the teacher is mostly grading who has the fastest friends. Take the points away and copying loses its purpose. The only person a copier would fool is themselves, right before the test that actually counts.

Then there is fairness. Homework happens at home, and homes are not equal. One student has a quiet desk, a parent who still remembers algebra, and an evening with nothing else on it. Another works at a grocery store until nine, or watches two younger siblings, or shares one laptop with three people. When homework counts, part of every grade quietly turns into a score for those conditions. A test taken in class at least puts everyone in the same room with the same clock.

The strongest argument on the other side is motivation. If homework doesn't count, the thinking goes, students will skip it and then bomb the tests. I take this seriously, because I know people who would skip it the first week. But points are a weak fix. A better one is to make the link between practice and results impossible to miss. Teachers can go over homework in class, give quick feedback, and build short quizzes straight from the assigned problems. A student who skips the practice and then fails a quiz on those exact problems learns something real about how studying works. That lesson sticks longer than a zero ever did.

A grade is supposed to tell a student, a parent, and eventually a college what someone has learned. Counting homework muddies that message with borrowed answers, family circumstances, and penalties for honest mistakes. Keeping it as practice protects the grade and gives students one place where getting something wrong costs nothing. School already has plenty of moments where performance is on the line. Homework should be the place where trying is enough.
