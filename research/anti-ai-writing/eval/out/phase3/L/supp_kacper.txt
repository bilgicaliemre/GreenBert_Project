People see the wrench and assume mechanical. But you can understand a scooter mechanically in an hour. I've had 41 apart since summer 2024, broken Xiaomis and Ninebots I buy on OLX, fix in the bike room under my building in Gdańsk, and resell. What takes longer is the controller and the battery board. That's why I want electrical engineering. It's also what I'm worst at.

In March I rebuilt a battery pack for Oleh, a Glovo courier, with salvaged cells, and soldered the strips because a spot welder cost 400 złoty. Nineteen days later it cut out at night in the rain. Two days after that I found my cracked joint and the cell I'd cooked beside it. That winter I'd argued online for three days that soldering cells is fine if you're fast. Now I own a spot welder and a fire extinguisher, and I don't do batteries for other people. I've backed away from the exact thing I want to study. I'd like to learn it properly.

What I'd bring: a notebook logging every repair, which says I make about seven złoty an hour. Żabka pays more, so it isn't only money. A seven-minute-forty inner tube change. An ear for worn folding hooks. And a lesson that cost me 150 złoty: open it up before you blame the rain.

Our building administrator kicks me out at ten. A friend's cousin sent a video of students building electric go-karts at 1 a.m., and nobody kicks them out.
