Cafeteria Swaps Foam for Plastic, but Third Lunch Gets the Wet Trays

The foam trays are gone. Since Monday, Oct. 5, the Quillmere Valley High School cafeteria has served lunch on hard plastic trays that get washed in the kitchen dishwasher instead of thrown in the trash.

The only notice was a flyer posted by the cafeteria door on Friday, Oct. 2. Several students the Ledger asked said they never saw it.

Cafeteria manager Renata Valdoro-Pike, who has worked at QVHS for 11 years, said the waste pushed her to make the change. "We were filling a dumpster with foam every week. I couldn't look at it anymore," she said in an interview on Oct. 14.

The cafeteria serves about 1,150 lunches a day over three lunch periods. Foam trays cost about six cents each. Valdoro-Pike estimates that added up to roughly $12,400 over a 180-day school year.

The school bought 1,400 reusable trays from Brindlemark Foodservice Supply for $4,620, or $3.30 a tray. Valdoro-Pike confirmed that total in an email on Oct. 16. A district "facilities sustainability" grant paid for the trays, and lunch still costs $3.25. A return cart and an extra dish rack cost another $1,180.

The kitchen also added a part-time dishwasher shift of two hours a day. Valdoro-Pike would not say what the shift pays, only that it is "in the budget."

By her count, the trash is shrinking. The cafeteria used to fill about 22 trash bags a day. Over the first eight school days of the new trays, Oct. 5 through Oct. 14, that dropped to about nine.

Not every tray has come back. As of Oct. 14, 37 were missing. "If you have one in your locker, just bring it back. No questions," Valdoro-Pike said.

Junior Theo Marchetti-Ross, who writes sports for the Ledger, likes the new trays. "They don't bend when you put the chili on them. That alone is worth it," he said.

Senior Priya Sandoval-Hune does not. "Third lunch gets the trays that are still wet. Nobody wants a wet tray," she said.

That complaint points to the one problem still unsolved. After second lunch, the dishwasher can't keep up, so third lunch, which starts at 12:40, gets damp trays. During third lunch on Thursday, Oct. 15, about one in four trays this reporter picked up felt damp. Around 1:05, roughly eight people waited in line at the return cart. Two students stacked their trays with food still on them. One tray fell, cracked at the corner, and went back in the rack anyway.

Valdoro-Pike said she is "looking at" ordering 200 more trays. She gave no date or cost, and the purchase would need district approval.
