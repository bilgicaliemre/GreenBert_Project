For two years one corner of the bike room in our basement has been mine: two shelves, a folding table from Lidl, one socket by the door. Since summer 2024 I've bought 41 broken e-scooters on OLX, fixed them there and resold them as KacperFix (4.9 stars, 37 reviews). My notebook says about eleven thousand złoty of profit. Once I divided it by my hours and got around seven złoty an hour, less than a job at Żabka would pay, so it's not only the money, probably, though I haven't figured out what else it is.

In March Oleh, a Ukrainian who delivers for Glovo, brought me his Xiaomi Pro 2 because the battery was dying on hills. "I need it for work, it must be good," he said. One group of ten cells was at 2.9 volts while the rest were almost at 4. I replaced that group with salvaged cells and tested them. Two came out lower capacity, and I thought, it's fine, it's a mix. New cells would have eaten my margin. I knew he needed the scooter for work, and I put the used ones in anyway. Both were true at the same time. I didn't have a spot welder (they cost 400 złoty), so I soldered the strips. Oleh paid 380 złoty, and I gave him a card I'd written by hand: "gwarancja 3 miesiące," three months' warranty.

Nineteen days later, at 22:10, the scooter cut out on Grunwaldzka, in the rain, with an order still in his bag. He pushed it about four kilometers to my building and came down to the bike room almost at midnight, wet. When he walked in, I was thinking about the 380, calculating. I can hear a worn folding hook before a rider says hello, and that night I said "it got wet" before I unscrewed anything. Then I opened it while he watched. There was a bit of water in the bottom (there always is, it's Gdańsk), and I pointed at it. I gave him back 150 złoty and said that was fair, because of the water. He said "dobra," okay, and walked out talking to someone on the phone in Ukrainian.

When I opened the pack properly, one of my solder joints was cracked, and I'd cooked the cell next to it. It was the repair, mine, not the rain, and it took me about two days to say that, even just in my head. Over the winter, in a Facebook group, I had argued for three days that you can solder cells if you're fast enough. I was on that side, and in April I deleted the comments.

A month later I bought a spot welder for 280 złoty. An 89-złoty fire extinguisher now hangs by the socket. And I don't do batteries for other people anymore. I know that sounds like a lesson, but it isn't. I just stopped doing the part where I can mess up. People see the wrench and assume mechanical engineering, but you understand a scooter mechanically in an hour. I think I want electrical, because the controller and the battery board are the interesting part, and also the part I'm worst at. So I've stopped doing, for other people, the thing I want to study, and I don't know what that is.

What I'd still like to solve is the rest of Oleh's money, 230 złoty. His number is still in my phone as "Oleh Pro2 Glovo." In June I saw him on Grunwaldzka on a new Ninebot, not one of mine, and I didn't wave. He never left a review on OLX, and I still check sometimes. I have the 230. I've opened BLIK to send it about four times, and every time there's the message box, and I'd have to write something in it, and what do I write?
