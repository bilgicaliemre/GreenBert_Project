I fix e-scooters in our building's basement. Oleh, a Glovo courier, brought me one with a dying battery: "I need it for work, it must be good." New cells would have eaten my margin, so I soldered in salvaged ones. I had no spot welder. I knew he needed it for work and used them anyway.

Nineteen days later it cut out in the rain, and he pushed it four kilometers to my building. I was calculating the 380 złoty he'd paid. I said "it got wet" before unscrewing anything and gave him back 150.

When I opened the pack properly, one of my solder joints had cracked and I had cooked the cell beside it. It took me two days to say, even in my head, that it was my repair and not the rain.

Now I own a spot welder, and I don't do batteries for other people. You understand a scooter mechanically in an hour. That half I can bring: I change an M365 inner tube in seven minutes forty. The controller and battery board are the interesting part, and what I'm worst at, so I think I want electrical engineering. Batteries, the part I stopped doing, are what I want to study, and I don't know what to make of that.

Oleh is still in my phone as "Oleh Pro2 Glovo." I've opened BLIK four times to send him the other 230. The problem is the message box, and what I'd write in it.
