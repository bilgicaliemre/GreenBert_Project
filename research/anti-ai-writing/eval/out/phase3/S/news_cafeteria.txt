Reusable trays cut cafeteria trash, but third lunch gets the wet ones

Foam lunch trays are gone from the Quillmere Valley High School cafeteria. Since Monday, Oct. 5, students have eaten off hard plastic trays that are washed in the kitchen dishwasher and used again.

The only notice was a flyer by the cafeteria door, put up the Friday before. Several students the Ledger asked said they never saw it.

"We were filling a dumpster with foam every week. I couldn't look at it anymore," said cafeteria manager Renata Valdoro-Pike, who has worked at QVHS for 11 years.

By her count, the cafeteria now sends out about nine trash bags a day, down from about 22 before the switch. The count covers the first eight school days with the new trays, Oct. 5 to 14.

The cafeteria serves about 1,150 lunches a day over three lunch periods. At about 6 cents per foam tray, Valdoro-Pike estimates the old trays cost about $12,400 a year, based on 180 school days. The cafeteria bought 1,400 reusable trays from Brindlemark Foodservice Supply for $4,620, or $3.30 each, with money from a district "facilities sustainability" grant, and lunch still costs $3.25. A return cart and an extra dish rack cost another $1,180.

The kitchen also added a part-time dishwasher shift of two hours a day. Valdoro-Pike would not say what the shift pays, only that it is "in the budget," so the Ledger could not work out whether the switch saves money.

Junior Theo Marchetti-Ross, who writes sports for the Ledger, likes the new trays. "They don't bend when you put the chili on them. That alone is worth it," he said.

Senior Priya Sandoval-Hune does not. "Third lunch gets the trays that are still wet," she said. "Nobody wants a wet tray." The dishwasher can't keep up after second lunch, so some trays are still damp at third lunch, which starts at 12:40.

During third lunch on Thursday, Oct. 15, a Ledger reporter saw about eight people in line at the return cart around 1:05. Two students stacked their trays with food still on them. One tray fell, cracked at a corner and went back in the rack anyway. About one in four trays the reporter picked up felt damp.

Valdoro-Pike said she is "looking at" 200 more trays. She gave no date or cost, and the extra trays would need district approval. The student council has mentioned compost bins, "maybe spring," but no school official has confirmed it.

As of Oct. 14, 37 trays were missing. "If you have one in your locker, just bring it back. No questions," Valdoro-Pike said.
