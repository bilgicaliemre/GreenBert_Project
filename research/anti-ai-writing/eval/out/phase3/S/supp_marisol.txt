My brother Gabe's math on our fridge whiteboard says my mom makes $4.10 an hour at our tamale stand on a normal Sunday. She has a disc thing in her back, and he wants her to quit the weekly markets.

At the July 18th night market, I sat on the cooler checking radar instead of unloading the canopy sandbags. When dust came from the east, Gabe said let's pack up. I said the cell over the Rincons was splitting north. "Trust me." My mom laughed: "La científica says we stay." At 6:52 the gust front cartwheeled our canopy into Mr. Duarte's honey table, and six dozen tamales went into the gravel.

The rain did go north. What hit us was the outflow I'd read about all summer, the wind that runs ahead of a storm. When Gabe said pack up, I thought: if we leave and it misses, that's one more point for his whiteboard. I just wanted to win.

Gabe told me I want the stand to stay exactly the same so it's still there when I come back, like my nana's chair. I said that was stupid. But I'm the one leaving, probably, to study where monsoon storms start, and the one saying keep standing on the gravel every Sunday, with her back.

In September my mom asked me, not Gabe, whether to drop the Saturday market next summer. I've done the numbers, in a tab next to my rain data, and I haven't answered her yet.
