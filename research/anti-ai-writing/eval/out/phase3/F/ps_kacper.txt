Oleh came down to the bike room almost at midnight, wet. He delivers for Glovo, and at 22:10 his Xiaomi Pro 2 had cut out on Grunwaldzka in the rain, with an order still in his bag. He pushed it about four kilometers to our building. Nineteen days earlier I had fixed its battery, taken 380 złoty and handed him a card I wrote by hand: "gwarancja 3 miesiące," three months' warranty. Before I unscrewed anything, I said, "It got wet."

In my head I was calculating the 380. I opened the pack while he watched, and there was a bit of water in the bottom (there always is; this is Gdańsk), so I pointed at it. I gave him back 150 złoty and said that was fair because of the water. He said "dobra," okay, and walked out talking on the phone in Ukrainian; I don't know what he said.

For two years one corner of that bike room has been mine: two shelves, a folding table from Lidl, one socket by the door. I buy broken e-scooters on OLX, fix them and sell them as KacperFix. In Oleh's battery one group of cells sat at 2.9 volts while the rest were almost at 4, so I replaced them with cells taken from a scooter I'd bought in Chełm. Two of those tested lower in capacity on my Liitokala charger, and I thought, it's fine, it's a mix. I soldered the strips, because a spot welder costs 400 złoty. In winter, on the Facebook group Hulajnogi Trójmiasto, I had argued for three days that you can solder cells if you're fast enough.

Later I opened the pack properly. One of my solder joints had cracked, and I had cooked the cell next to it. It was the repair, mine, not the rain. Saying that, even just in my head, took me two days, and in April I deleted my comments from the group.

When Oleh first brought the scooter in, he told me, "I need it for work, it must be good." I put the salvaged cells in anyway, because new ones would have eaten my margin. I knew both things at the same time. My notebook says 41 scooters since summer 2024, about eleven thousand złoty of profit. Once I divided that by my hours and got around seven złoty an hour, less than Żabka pays. So it's probably not only about money, though I haven't figured out what else it is. But at the two moments that counted with Oleh, choosing the cells and watching him walk in wet, I was thinking about money.

A month later I bought a spot welder from AliExpress for 280 złoty. A fire extinguisher from Castorama, 89 złoty, hangs by the socket. And I stopped doing batteries for other people. I know that sounds like a lesson, but I just stopped doing the part where I can mess up, which is also the part I want to study, and I don't know what that is. The wrench part is boring; you understand a scooter mechanically in an hour. The controller and the battery board are what's interesting, the reason I think I want electrical engineering, and also what I'm worst at.

What I haven't solved is the other 230 złoty. I have the money. I've opened BLIK to send it about four times, and there's the message box, where I'd have to write something. It probably starts with what I didn't say at midnight: it was the repair, mine, not the rain. After that I don't know. In June I saw him on Grunwaldzka on a new Ninebot, not one of mine, and I didn't wave. He never left a review on OLX, and I still check sometimes. His number is still in my phone as "Oleh Pro2 Glovo."
