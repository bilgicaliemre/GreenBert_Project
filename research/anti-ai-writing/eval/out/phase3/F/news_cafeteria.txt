Reusable trays cut cafeteria trash, but third lunch gets damp ones

The cafeteria replaced its foam trays with reusable ones on Monday, Oct. 5. In the first eight school days, it threw out about nine bags of trash a day, down from about 22 before the switch, according to a count by cafeteria manager Renata Valdoro-Pike. Students at third lunch, though, have been getting trays that are still damp.

The old foam trays went into the trash after one use. The new ones are hard plastic and are washed in the kitchen dishwasher. The only notice of the change was a flyer by the cafeteria door, posted Friday, Oct. 2, and several students the Ledger asked said they never saw it.

"We were filling a dumpster with foam every week," said Valdoro-Pike, who has worked at Quillmere Valley for 11 years. "I couldn't look at it anymore." The cafeteria serves about 1,150 lunches a day over three lunch periods, and each foam tray cost about 6 cents. She estimates that foam came to about $12,400 a year, based on 180 school days.

The 1,400 new trays came from Brindlemark Foodservice Supply and cost $4,620, or $3.30 each, a figure Valdoro-Pike confirmed by email. A district "facilities sustainability" grant paid for them, and lunch still costs $3.25. A return cart and an extra dish rack cost another $1,180.

The switch also added a two-hour part-time dishwasher shift each day. Valdoro-Pike would not say what the shift pays, only that it is "in the budget," so the Ledger could not work out whether the new trays save money overall.

Junior Theo Marchetti-Ross likes them. "They don't bend when you put the chili on them. That alone is worth it," he said. Marchetti-Ross writes sports for the Ledger.

Senior Priya Sandoval-Hune does not. "Third lunch gets the trays that are still wet. Nobody wants a wet tray," she said. Third lunch starts at 12:40, and the dishwasher cannot keep up after second lunch.

A Ledger reporter watched third lunch on Thursday, Oct. 15. Around 1:05 p.m., about eight people were waiting at the return cart. Two students stacked their trays with food still on them. One tray was dropped and cracked at a corner, and it went back in the rack anyway. About one in four of the trays the reporter picked up felt damp.

Valdoro-Pike said she is "looking at" adding 200 more trays, but there is no date or cost for that yet, and the purchase would need district approval.

As of Oct. 14, 37 trays were missing. Valdoro-Pike's request to whoever has them: "If you have one in your locker, just bring it back. No questions."
