Oleh, a Glovo courier, brought his Pro 2 to the basement bike room where I fix scooters. One cell group was dead. "I need it for work," he said. "It must be good."

I knew that and still used salvaged cells, because new ones would eat my margin. I soldered the strips and charged him 380 złoty.

Nineteen days later it cut out in the rain, and he pushed it about four kilometers to me. Thinking about the 380, I said "it got wet" before unscrewing anything, pointed at a little water inside (there always is, it's Gdańsk) and gave back 150.

It took about two days to say, even in my head, that it was my repair: a solder joint had cracked, and I'd cooked the cell beside it. I'd spent about three days on a Facebook group arguing you can solder cells if you're fast enough.

I want engineering, electrical I think, because the controller and battery board are the interesting part (and what I'm worst at). Since Oleh, I don't do other people's batteries. It sounds like a lesson, but I just stopped doing the part where I can mess up, which is the part I want to study.

What I'd bring is 41 scooters fixed since summer 2024, and the one that came back. Oleh's number is still in my phone, and I have the other 230 złoty. I've opened BLIK about four times, but I don't know what to write in the message box.
