Reusable trays replace foam, but third lunch gets the damp ones

Foam trays are gone from the Quillmere Valley High School cafeteria. Since Monday, Oct. 5, lunch has come on hard plastic trays that are washed in the kitchen dishwasher and reused, and students in third lunch are getting some of them before they dry.

The only notice of the switch was a flyer by the cafeteria door, posted Friday, Oct. 2. Several students the Ledger asked said they never saw it.

Cafeteria manager Renata Valdoro-Pike has worked at QVHS for 11 years. "We were filling a dumpster with foam every week. I couldn't look at it anymore," she said in an interview on Oct. 14.

The cafeteria serves about 1,150 lunches a day over three lunch periods, Valdoro-Pike said. Foam trays cost about 6 cents each, which she estimates came to roughly $12,400 a year over 180 school days.

The 1,400 reusable trays came from Brindlemark Foodservice Supply at $3.30 each, or $4,620 in all, a total Valdoro-Pike confirmed by email on Oct. 16. A district "facilities sustainability" grant paid for the trays, and lunch still costs $3.25. A return cart and an extra dish rack cost another $1,180.

Washing the trays takes an extra part-time dishwasher shift of two hours a day. Valdoro-Pike would not say what that shift pays, only that it is "in the budget."

By her count, the cafeteria used to put out about 22 bags of trash a day. In the first eight school days with the new trays, Oct. 5 through 14, it put out about nine.

Junior Theo Marchetti-Ross, who writes sports for the Ledger, likes the change. "They don't bend when you put the chili on them. That alone is worth it," he said.

The dishwasher can't keep up after second lunch, so third lunch, which starts at 12:40, gets trays that are still damp. "Third lunch gets the trays that are still wet. Nobody wants a wet tray," said senior Priya Sandoval-Hune.

During third lunch on Thursday, Oct. 15, about one in four trays this reporter picked up felt damp. Around 1:05 p.m., about eight people were waiting at the return cart. Two students stacked trays with food still on them, and one tray was dropped, cracked at a corner and went back into the rack anyway.

Valdoro-Pike said she is "looking at" buying 200 more trays. She has no date or cost yet, and the purchase would need district approval.

As of Oct. 14, 37 trays were missing. "If you have one in your locker, just bring it back. No questions," Valdoro-Pike said.
