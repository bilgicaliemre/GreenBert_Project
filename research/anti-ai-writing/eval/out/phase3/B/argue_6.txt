Homework Should Not Count

One middle option for homework is to let it count, but only a little: say a fifth of the grade, maybe scored just for turning it in. It looks fair: students get a reason to practice, and a wrong answer on a worksheet doesn't sink anyone. I don't think it works, because the teacher has no way to know who did the work behind even those few points. Homework should be practice only. Teachers should still assign it and go over it in class, but it shouldn't count toward the grade.

Suppose homework is 20 percent of the grade and tests are the other 80. One student does every assignment herself and gets about 70 percent of the problems right, since she's still learning the material when she does them. Another student copies a friend's answers and scores 100. On tests, the first student averages 85 and the second averages 80. Her final grade is 82 (14 points from homework, 68 from tests). His is 84 (20 plus 64). The tests say she knows more, and the report card says the opposite. Her homework was also more useful to the teacher, because her wrong answers showed which problems to go back over, and his perfect page showed nothing.

Grading only for completion fixes that math, since both students would get full credit. But then the points don't measure understanding at all. They measure whether a page came back, and a copied page comes back as easily as a worked one. If one student has an older sister who took the same class and another works evening shifts and can't always finish, the second one is the student who loses points, even if she understands the material just as well. A test in class gives both of them the same hour and the same rules.

The best argument for grading homework is that students won't do it otherwise. That's partly right. A student with a job, a sport and five other classes is likely to drop whatever counts for nothing first, and practice only helps the people who do it. So the rule needs a consequence, but one that a copied page can't buy. Homework for a unit could be the ticket to retaking that unit's test: a student who wants a second try brings the finished practice first. The homework still earns no points, so copying it can't raise a grade, and a student who copies just to qualify still has to pass the retake alone.

A second argument for counting homework is that it protects students who understand the material but freeze on tests. That problem is real, but homework points give the same cushion to a student who copied, so they don't show which student is which. The retakes help a student who froze, and so do in-class writing and explaining a solution to the teacher out loud. Each of those shows what a student understands without one timed test deciding everything, and the teacher can see who did the work.

Taking homework out of the grade would move some report cards. The student in the example who did her own work would go up. A student whose homework points were covering for low test scores would go down, and that student's parents might reasonably be upset. But the higher grade was telling them something the tests didn't back up, and the retakes and in-class options give that student a way to earn the grade back with work the teacher can see.
