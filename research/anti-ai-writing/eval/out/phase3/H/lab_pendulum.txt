Discussion

Our results support the model T = 2π√(L/g). The graph of T² against L is a straight line with a slope of 4.105 s²/m and an intercept of about −0.002 s², effectively zero, so the line passes through the origin. From the slope, g = 4π²/4.105 = 9.62 m/s², about 1.9% below the accepted value of 9.81 m/s².

The error is systematic. All five mean periods were about 1% longer than predicted; at 1.00 m the formula gives 2.006 s and we measured 2.026 s. Since g depends on T², a 1% error in the period becomes roughly a 2% error in g, which matches our result.

The string is the most likely cause. The proper string had run out, so we used cotton twine, and it stretched: the 1.00 m length measured about 1.01 m after a few minutes loaded. Using 1.01 m for that point gives g ≈ 9.71 m/s², closing about half the gap. We did not re-measure the other lengths, so I cannot say how much each stretched, but if every real length was longer than the recorded one, every period would come out too long, which is what we saw.

Timing fits the pattern less well. My reaction time is about 0.2 s and the spread within each length was at most 0.15 s over 10 swings, but random errors like these push some periods up and others down. Jonah thinks I started the stopwatch late a couple of times; a late start shortens the measured time, so it cannot explain periods that are too long. The release angle may have added a little. Below 0.60 m it was estimated by eye, and wider swings take slightly longer than the formula predicts.

We left out one trial. At 0.60 m, trial 2 (14.11 s) was almost certainly 9 swings instead of 10, because Jonah lost count when Mr. Pell came in. Dividing by 9 gives 1.568 s, nearly the same as the 1.569 s mean of the other two trials. With it included, that point sits well below the line and R² drops from 1.000 to 0.997, though the slope barely changes.

Next time we would use a less stretchy string and measure each length with the bob already hanging. We would also use the protractor at every length and try Jonah's phone light-gate app, which would remove reaction-time and counting errors.
