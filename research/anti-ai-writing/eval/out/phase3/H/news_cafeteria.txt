Foam trays are gone, but third lunch is still waiting for dry ones

Foam lunch trays are gone from the Quillmere Valley High School cafeteria. Since Monday, Oct. 5, students have been eating off hard plastic trays that get washed in the kitchen dishwasher. The foam ones went in the trash.

The only notice of the change was a flyer posted by the cafeteria door on Friday, Oct. 2. Several students the Ledger asked said they never saw it.

"We were filling a dumpster with foam every week. I couldn't look at it anymore," said cafeteria manager Renata Valdoro-Pike, who has worked at QVHS for 11 years. She spoke with the Ledger in her office on Oct. 14.

The cafeteria serves about 1,150 lunches a day over three lunch periods. At about 6 cents a tray, Valdoro-Pike estimates foam cost roughly $12,400 a year, based on 180 school days.

The 1,400 reusable trays came from Brindlemark Foodservice Supply and cost $4,620, or $3.30 each. A district "facilities sustainability" grant paid for them, and lunch still costs $3.25. A return cart and an extra dish rack cost another $1,180.

Washing the trays takes an extra part-time dishwasher shift of two hours a day. Valdoro-Pike would not say what the shift pays, only that it is "in the budget."

By her count, the cafeteria went from about 22 bags of trash a day to about nine during the program's first eight school days, Oct. 5 through Oct. 14.

Junior Theo Marchetti-Ross, who writes sports for the Ledger, likes the new trays.

"They don't bend when you put the chili on them. That alone is worth it," he said.

Senior Priya Sandoval-Hune does not. "Third lunch gets the trays that are still wet. Nobody wants a wet tray," she said.

That problem has not been solved. The dishwasher can't keep up after second lunch, so third lunch, which starts at 12:40, gets trays that are still damp. Valdoro-Pike said she is "looking at" buying 200 more trays, but there is no date or cost yet, and the purchase would need district approval.

A Ledger reporter watched the return cart during third lunch on Thursday, Oct. 15. Around 1:05 p.m., about eight people were waiting in line there, and two students stacked trays with food still on them. One tray was dropped and cracked at the corner, then went back into the rack anyway. About one in four trays the reporter picked up felt damp.

Some trays have also gone missing: 37 of them as of Oct. 14.

"If you have one in your locker, just bring it back. No questions," Valdoro-Pike said.
