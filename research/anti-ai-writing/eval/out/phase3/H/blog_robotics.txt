Regional recap: Ostrander Valley, October 17

Last Saturday, fourteen of us went to the Ostrander Valley Robotics League regional at Marrowfield Community College, along with our two mentors and Coach Tibbetts. We went 3-2-1 in qualifications, ranked 9th of 32, got picked for an alliance, and lost in the quarterfinals. We didn't win any awards and didn't qualify for state. Our next chance is the Elk Ridge qualifier on November 14.

The bus was supposed to leave at 5:45 and left at 6:10. Rohan Achterberg forgot the battery charger, so the driver turned around. At 8:15 we failed inspection because our bumper was 2mm too high. We filed it down and passed at 8:50.

Qualification matches, our score first:

- Q4: lost 48-61.
- Q11: won 72-40. Our autonomous was mirrored wrong for the red side, and the robot drove straight into the wall at the start. We won anyway.
- Q19: tied 55-55. That one's on me. I misread the schedule, and the queuer called our number twice before we showed up.
- Q23: lost 30-68. The shoulder bracket on our intake arm cracked, and the arm hung limp for the rest of the match.
- Q31: won 81-52, our best score of the day.
- Q38: won 64-59, even with the left drive motor overheating near the end.

I printed that bracket Thursday night at 20% infill to save time. It should have been 100%, or metal. Marisol Fenwick, a sophomore, and our mentor Gus Abernathy-Reyes swapped in an aluminum bracket from the spares. It took them 25 minutes, and the arm was fixed before Q31. Marisol cut her finger on the edge, got a band-aid, and was fine.

Team 4410, the Voltage Vultures from Copperline Prep, were an alliance captain and picked us second. At alliance selection Theo Brannagan wore the fox head, couldn't see, and walked into Copperline's pit banner and knocked it over. Their whole pit laughed, and Theo bowed. The quarterfinals were best of three. We lost 0-2, 71-77 and 60-84.

In our pit interview, the judge (Ms. Ferraro-Quint, according to her name tag) said our notebook was the best organized she'd seen that day. She also pointed out that our CAD screenshots had no dimensions, and she asked why we had 3D-printed parts on load-bearing joints. I didn't have a good answer for her.

On the bus home we argued about what to do before Elk Ridge, and it got loud. Rohan and I want to rebuild the whole arm in aluminum. Marisol and Theo want to reprint at 100% and spend the time on autonomous. Coach Tibbetts said we'll decide at Tuesday's meeting.

For the record, the gym AC was broken and it was about 84°F inside. The vending machine ate Priya's $2, our driver Mr. Lund kept the same classic rock station on all day, and Culver's beat Wendy's 9-5 in the food-stop vote. I wore the wrong shoes, and my feet hurt by noon.

Thank you to Quillby's Hardware for donating our aluminum stock.

Dana Okafor-Lindqvist, mechanical lead
