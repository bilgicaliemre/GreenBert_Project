At 3 p.m. in monsoon season, everybody in Tucson watches the mountains to see where storms will start. The models are bad at predicting that, and it's what I want to study.

My uncle Memo gave me the Raspberry Pi for my rooftop weather station. That first June it logged 19 rainy days, though it hadn't rained once; the swamp cooler was dripping in. My dad checks its heat index before his mail route and calls it "Marisol's channel."

Weekends belong to my mom's tamale stand. My nana Chayo tells us from her chair that the masa is too thick, and I run the Square reader. At the July 18 night market, I watched radar instead of setting out our four sandbags. Dust came from the east, and my brother Gabe said to pack up. His fridge whiteboard shows my mom making $4.10 an hour on a normal Sunday, and I didn't want to hand him another losing night. I said, "Trust me." The rain did go north, but I'd forgotten the outflow wind I read about all summer. Our canopy cartwheeled into Mr. Duarte's honey table: fourteen jars, $168. My mom paid him from her apron.

Now the sandbags go down before I look at the sky. In September my mom asked me, not Gabe, whether to drop the Saturday market next summer. The numbers are done, in a tab next to my rain data. I haven't answered her yet, and I still owe her $108.
